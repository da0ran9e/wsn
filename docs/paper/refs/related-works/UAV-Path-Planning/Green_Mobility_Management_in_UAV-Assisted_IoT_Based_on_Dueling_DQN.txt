Green Mobility Management in UA V-Assisted IoT
Based on Dueling DQN
Wenqi Liu, Pengbo Si, Enchang Sun, Meng Li, Chao Fang, Yanhua Zhang
Faculty of Information Technology,
Beijing University of Technology
Beijing, China
lwqi@emails.bjut.edu.cn
Abstract —In most cases, the batteries of sensor nodes in the
Internet of Things (IoT) are usually constrained by size and
weight, and are difﬁcult to recharge or replace. In traditional
wireless sensor networks, data is transmitted in a multi-hopmanner, which may cause the high data transmission delay andunbalanced trafﬁc load. In this paper, an Unmanned Aerial
V ehicle (UA V)-assisted IoT architecture is introduced, in which
UA V is utilized to achieve low-latency and seamless-coverage
acquisition of the sensing data. Furthermore, based on the recent
advances on deep reinforcement learning algorithms, consideringboth data delay requirements and network energy consumption, areal-time ﬂight path planning scheme of the UA V in the dynamicIoT sensor networks has been proposed based on dueling deepQ-network (DQN). Besides, the grid-based method is used to
handle the network state modeling, which effectively reduces
the complexity of the proposed scheme. Simulation results show
that the proposed scheme signiﬁcantly improves the network
performance.
Index T erms—UA V-assisted IoT, green mobility management,
ﬂight path, deep reinforcement learning, grid-based method
I. I NTRODUCTION
As one of the most signiﬁcant advances in recent years
on communication and networking technologies, Internet of
Things (IoT) facilitates the interconnection among almosteverything for real-time and delay-tolerant information ex-
change. Speciﬁcally, IoT is playing an important role in
environment monitoring, agriculture, manufacturing, militaryscenarios, among others, thanks to its strong capability of
collecting the ﬁeld data for further big data analysis, intel-
ligent learning and decision making in data centers or edgecomputing devices [1].
The data collection part of IoT suffers from the similar
problem as traditional wireless sensor networks: the energyholes caused by the battery drain of IoT nodes which are
constrained by size and weight with limited battery capacity.
Fortunately, unmanned aerial vehicle (UA V) has attractedmuch attention and been intensively studied, due to its greatconvenience on deployment and programming [2][3]. Thecommunication devices loaded on UA Vs can be utilized as thebase stations or access points with controlled mobility to cover
the isolated data islands for effective data uploading, and to

UA Vs into IoT and forming UA V-assisted IoT, the network
disruption problem can be greatly solved [5].
In the UA V-assisted IoT, UA Vs act as mobile sinks (MS),
of which patrol path planning becomes one of the key issues
affecting the system performance. In [6], the authors propose astochastic compressive data collection protocol named SMITEfor mobile wireless sensor networks, the sensor nodes in thenetwork transmit the data to the aggregation nodes, and theMS moves randomly to collect the data from the aggregation
nodes. A grid-based load-balanced routing method (GLRM)
has been studied in [7]. The authors adopt the virtual grid-
based method, with which the forwarding nodes are electedaccording to the number of packets carried by the nodes,the Euclidean distance from the center of the grid, and theremaining energy of the nodes. MSs collect the data fromthe forwarding nodes according to the pre-set path. However,real-time path planning is ignored in both [6] and [7]. To solvethis problem, a weighted rendezvous planning (WRP) methodis proposed to select the Rendezvous Points (RPs), and the
mobile sink only visits the RPs for information collection [8].
The travelling salesman problem (TSP) algorithm is introduced
in this work to calculate the shortest path of the MS traversingthe RP nodes, thus reducing the data transmission delay. How-
ever, issues such as quality-of-service (QoS), computational
complexity, energy consumption, etc., are still open and maydramatically affect its application in real scenarios.
On the other hand, deep learning meets reinforcement learn-
ing and the resulting major breakthrough in machine learning
is already reshaping our world. Deep reinforcement learning(DRL) is widely applied as a generous-purpose algorithmwith strong capability of making optimal action decisionsand abstracting high-level features from complex scenes [9].
Google DeepMind team has proposed deep Q-network (DQN)
in 2015 [10], achieving results beyond human in Atari games.AlphaGo [11] and AlphaGo Zero [12] also achieve a very highlevel in Go games. After that, DeepMind team made furtherimprovements based on DQN, namely Double DQN [13],which establishes a target DQN that calculates the maximumQ value. Dueling DQN [14] is another major improvement.
It divides the Q-value function into two parts, representingthe value function of the environment state and the advantage

To achieve green UA V-assisted IoT, a layered network
architecture is introduced and a real-time UA V path planning
scheme is proposed in this paper. The mobility of the UA V iscontrolled by the moving direction action decisions to mini-mize power consumption while meeting the delay constraints.The system is formulated as a dynamic decision optimization
problem, which is solved by dueling DQN and grid-based
method. The main contributions of this paper are as follows.
•The decision sub-layer, the MS sub-layer and the con-vergence sub-layer are introduced in the UA V-assisted
IoT architecture to handle the machine learning, the UA V
management and the data aggregation issues, respectively.
•Considering the different data transmission delay con-
straints from different types of sensing nodes, a priority-based model is adopted. Besides, we use the grid-based
method to formulate the system as a discrete one.
•We introduce dueling DQN to optimally and efﬁciently
solve the real-time UA V path planning problem, anduse Google TensorFlow as the simulation platform todemonstrate the signiﬁcant performance improvement.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section II,
the network architecture of the UA V-assisted IoT is described.We present the system model in Section III. Then, in SectionIV , the problem of the UA V path planning is formulated as aDRL process. Simulation results are presented and discussedin Section V and conclusions are drawn in Section VI.
II. N
ETWORK ARCHITECTURE
A four-layer network architecture is introduced as in Fig.
1 in this section. Sensing layer, access layer, infrastructurelayer and application layer devices are connected via wiredor wireless channels to provide the internetworking of things,with UA Vs as mobile sinks.
A. Sensing Layer
Sensing layer in the network is responsible for sensing and
collecting environmental information or ﬁeld data, composed
of massive IoT information acquisition devices such as sen-sors, cameras, GPS devices, among others.
B. Access Layer
Access layer provides wireless access to the core networks
and the data processing services. Different from radio access
networks (RANs) in 5G and other cellular networks, the access
layer in UA V-assisted IoT systems can be divided into two sub-layers, i.e., convergence sub-layer and MS sub-layer. Collec-
tion points (CP) in the convergence sub-layer are selected from
the nodes in the sensing layer, responsible for collecting andaggregating the data from the sensing nodes. The aggregateddata is then transmitted to the upper MS sub-layer, which is
composed of access points with controlled mobility, such asUA Vs and unmanned ground vehicles (UGVs). These mobilesinks move around over the CP nodes, based on the pathsplanned in the real-time manner.C. Infrastructure Layer
Infrastructure layer in the network architecture is responsi-
ble for receiving various data collected by the access layer
and uploading the data to the application layer for furtherprocessing and storage. Communication satellites, 4G/5G base
stations, LoRa base stations, NB-IoT base stations and road-
side units (RSUs) are typical devices in the infrastructure layer.
D. Application Layer
User sub-layer in the application layer provides the platform
to support various user applications (APPs). The other sub-
layer in the application layer, decision sub-layer, facilitatesthe information processing and data analytics with machinelearning and big data techniques. The optimization of the UA V
path planning and CP node selecting is also executed in this
sub-layer. Note that the algorithms in the decision sub-layer
are deployed in the form of APPs as well.
E. Energy Consumption and Delay Issues
The IoT end terminals are generally small-scale devices
powered by batteries. Thus, dead devices may result in fault
routes and a disruptive network topology. Speciﬁcally, keepingthe CP nodes in the convergence sub-layer alive is signiﬁcantlyimportant due to their key function of collecting and aggre-gating the data from the sensing layer. UA Vs in the networkalso consume much energy, and their power optimization iskey to the system performance. Besides, due to the natureof complexity in IoT, different sensors may generate thedata packets with different delay constraints. In this case,computing and communication resources could be scheduledwith different priorities. Considering both energy consumption

III. S YSTEM MODEL
The system model including network model and energy
model is presented in this section.
A. Problem Description
For simplicity, the single MS scenario is considered in this
paper. Real-time path planning for the UA V in the system
is studied, taking into account the dynamic delay constraintsand the problem of energy holes. The randomly distributedsensor nodes in the sensing layer generate the sensing datapackets periodically, and the selected CP nodes upload thecollected data to the MS within its communication range. TheMS moves based on the path that is planned in the real-timemanner, and forwards the data to the application layer viabase stations or access points in the infrastructure layer. Pathplanning decisions are made at the servers in the decision sub-layer and then distributed to the MS. The main objective inthis paper is to minimize the power consumption of the CP
nodes while meeting the delay constraints, by the means of
optimizing of the UA V patrol paths.
B. Network Model
AnNm×Nm area is considered as the target area with the
coverage of the UA V-assisted IoT system. The area is divided
into grids. A CP node is selected in each grid to collect theinformation from the sensors in the grid, and transmit the datato the UA V . Here, direct communication among the nodes inone grid is assumed. Let the center of each grid to be the pointthat the UA V stays for receiving the data from the CP node.Thus, as shown in Fig. 2, a graph G(V,E )covering the target
area can be obtained by connecting all the center points of the
grids, where VandErepresent the center point of the grid
and the edge connecting the points, respectively. Besides, the
UA V can change its location to be above the center point ofany grid for data collection, along the edge of the graph G
with the step length l(e)at each time slot, where l(e)is the
length of edge e∈E.

VI. C
ONCLUSIONS
In this paper, considering the random distribution of sensing
nodes and the resulting unbalanced trafﬁc load in UA V-
assisted IoT systems, a green and real-time MS path planningscheme has been proposed, with the objective of reducingpower consumption while meeting the delay constraints fordifferent data types.
