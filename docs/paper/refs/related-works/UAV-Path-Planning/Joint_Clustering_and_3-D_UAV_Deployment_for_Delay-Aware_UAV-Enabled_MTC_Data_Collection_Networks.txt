Abstract—
 The design of timely data collection for a machine-type communication (MTC) net-
work by unmanned-aerial-vehicle (UAV) platform is investigated. The ground-based MTC devices
are clustered for efﬁcient service, and the UAV station’s deployment in the 3-D space is opti-
mized. The corresponding mission time minimization problem is formulated as a coupled mixed-
integer nonlinear program. For tractability, the original problem is decomposed into two sub-
problems respectively dealing with clustering-hovering optimization and intercluster UAV traveling
path minimization. An alternating clustering-hovering optimization (ACH) and ant colony optimiza-
tion (ACO) solution approach is proposed accordingly. Simulations are conducted to validate the
superiority of the proposed ACH–ACO scheme over the scheme based on k-means clustering.
Index Terms—Sensor networks, clustering, mission time minimization, UAV 3-D deployment, UAV-assisted data collection.
I. INTRODUCTION
As a key foundation of Internet-of-Things (IoT) applications,
machine-type communication (MTC) has been developing rapidly in
recent years [1]. By deploying a large number of machine-type com-
munication devices (MTCDs) to sense the environment and exchange
control messages, MTC networks can effectively support timely intel-
ligent management applications of information and physical systems,
such as autonomous driving and factory automation [2]. Central to
quality-of-service provisioning in such applications of MTC networks
is efﬁcient data collection from a large number of MTCDs with latency
considerations [3].
In conventional data collection schemes, the MTCDs transmit data
to a ground-based collector station directly or through relaying, which
is easily blocked by obstacles and energy-inefﬁcient. To overcome the
above issues, UA Vs have been introduced to establish airborne data
sinks to support more efﬁcient and ﬂexible data collection [4],[5].
Owing to its high mobility, a UA V can approach ground MTCDs and
provide strong Line-of-Sight (LoS) links. As a result, UA V-assisted
data collection can achieve much lower mission time than conventional
schemes based on ﬁxed ground stations.
There have been many works in the literature aiming at reducing
latency of UA V-assisted data collection through trajectory optimiza-
tion. For instance, in [6], trajectory optimization and data collection
algorithms were proposed for simultaneous shortening ﬂight time and
improving data collection rate. By jointly optimizing the UA V station’s
trajectory, ﬂying height, and velocity, a UA V-aided data collection de-
sign was proposed in [7]to minimize the total mission time. However, it
is challenging to accurately model the inﬂuence of the highly dynamic
UA V ﬂight attitude on wireless transmission, so the above works ignore
the impact of UA V ﬂight attitude on communication. Noting that the

On the other hand, due to the limited coverage and capacity of
UA V stations, it is reasonable to serve a large number of MTCDs in
a clustered manner. However, joint design of MTCD clustering and
UA V hovering is a complex coupled optimization problem, which has
yet been adequately studied. In [10], the ground MTCDs were ﬁrst
clustered, the UA V then hovers above each cluster during its ﬂight
for data collection. Since the UA V hovering positions are obtained
through weighted k-means principle, the scheme has no optimality
guarantee. A dual-hop design that used a cluster head to gather data
from close-by MTCDs and then forwarded it to the UA V station was
investigated in [11]. This two-hop process had a signiﬁcant additional
overhead, and hence is undesirable for IoT applications.
In this letter, we consider minimization of the overall mission time
in a UA V-assisted MTC data collection network by jointly optimizing
MTCD clustering, UA V hovering, and UA V ﬂying strategies in a
3-D space. The problem is formulated as a coupled mixed-integer
nonlinear program (MINLP). To tackle the coupled issue, the prob-
lem is separated into two subproblems, which respectively optimize
the clustering-hovering strategy and the intercluster ﬂying strategy.
The former determines the communication/hovering time, and the
latter determines the traveling time. An alternating clustering-hovering
optimization and ant colony optimization (ACH–ACO) solution ap-
proach is proposed. Speciﬁcally, ACH is responsible for clustering-
hovering optimization, and ACO designs intercluster ﬂying trajecto-
ries. Through numerical examples, the proposed scheme is shown to
effectively reduce the overall mission time.

VI. CONCLUSION
Delay-aware design of UA V-enabled data collection for MTC net-
works has been studied, with a focus on clustering of the MTCDs
and deployment of the UA V in the 3-D space. The total mission
time minimization problem has been formulated as a challenging
MINLP program. Based on the assumption of communication-only-
when-hovering operation, the original problem has been decomposed
into two tractable subproblems, which, respectively, deal with MTCD
clustering and UA V hovering position optimization and intercluster
ﬂying path minimization. An ACH algorithm that alternately optimizes
MTCD clustering and in-cluster UA V hovering positions has been
proposed to solve the former subproblem, and the latter subproblem is
readily solved by the ACO algorithm. Numerical results have shown
quick convergence of the ACH process in obtaining the MTCD clus-
tering and UA V hovering strategy that minimizes the hovering time.
Total mission time of the proposed ACH–ACO approach has been
examined and compared with the strategy based on k-means clustering
in numerical examples. It has been observed that the ACH–ACO
algorithm signiﬁcantly outperforms the kmeans-based strategy in
terms of total mission time, and the improvement is greater when the
number of MTCDs and the mission area are larger.
