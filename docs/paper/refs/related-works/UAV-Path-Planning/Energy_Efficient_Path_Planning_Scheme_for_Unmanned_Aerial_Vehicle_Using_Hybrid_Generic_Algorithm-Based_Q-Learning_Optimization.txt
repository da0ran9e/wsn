ABSTRACT Efficient path planning optimization strategies are required to maximize flying time while
consuming the least energy. This research offers a novel approach for energy-efficient path planning
for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UA Vs) that combines a hybrid evolutionary algorithm and Q-learning
while accounting for the UA V’s velocity and distance from obstacles. To overcome the constraints of
traditional optimization approaches, the hybrid methodology combines genetic algorithms and Q-learning.
The suggested approach optimizes path-planning decisions based on real-time information by considering
the UA V’s velocity and distance from obstacles. Genetic Algorithm (GA) creates a wide collection of
candidate pathways. In contrast, Q-learning uses reinforcement learning to make educated selections based
on the UA V’s present velocity and proximity to static obstacles. This integration allows the UA V to modify
its path dynamically based on its energy requirements and environmental constraints. The main goal is
to develop a UA V path planning scheme capable of dealing with obstacle-filled environments to improve
energy efficiency and collision avoidance during flight missions. Our experimental results show that the
hybrid technique outperforms the classical GA method in terms of energy efficiency by significantly
reducing energy consumption while maintaining a suitable collision rate and the best path cost to the desired
locations. The analysis results improve the performance of the hybrid GA/QL algorithm by more than 57.14%
compared to classical GA.

II. TECHNICAL BACKGROUND
Much research has recently been done to address the issues
of optimizing energy-efficient path planning for unmanned
aerial vehicles (UA Vs). Researchers have investigated various
ways, including merging classical algorithms with mod-
ern optimization techniques and artificial intelligence (AI)
methodologies [5]. Most combination approaches attempt to
build algorithms capable of adapting to dynamic situations,
avoiding obstacles, optimizing velocity control, and coordi-
nating many UA Vs [6]. These approaches seek to improve
energy efficiency, mission capabilities, and the ability of
UA Vs to function effectively in various settings.
Recent studies have shown that swarm algorithms (SA) and
machine learning (ML) algorithms can help UA V plan their
paths more energy-efficient manner. These methods enable
efficient computing while navigating complex settings. Addi-
tionally, these methods can be integrated to boost UA V
path planning’s energy efficiency and process optimization.
Poudel et al., 2021 describe a hybrid path planning (HPP)
algorithm for effective data collection by assuring the shortest
collision-free path for UA V emergencies. The probabilistic
roadmap (PRM) technique creates the shortest trajectory map
in the proposed HPP scheme. The optimized artificial bee
colony (ABC) approach is used to better distinct path restric-
tions in a three-dimensional environment. Regarding flight
time, energy consumption, convergence time, and flight path,
the findings of the results reveal that the proposed HPP beats
the PRM and standard ABC schemes significantly.
Yuwen et al. 2021 provide the deep learning trained by GA
(DL-GA) technique for addressing the issues of multi-UA V
path planning in difficult settings. The authors emphasize
the need for well-planned pathways to lower UA V numbers
and energy consumption. The innovative DL-GA approach
combines the GA and DL taking use of the speed and
problem-solving skills of DL. GA takes data from many
circumstances to train a deep neural network. This results in
quick path optimization, especially suitable for scenarios withtight deadlines. The experimental findings show that DL-GA
beats GA in solving speed without sacrificing optimization
capacity.
Wang et al. 2022 consider the UA V’s energy usage and
the data gathering model. The proposed study aims to create
a multi-objective UA V path planning model using improved
particle swarm optimization and genetic algorithms. It sug-
gests a hybrid method (IPSO-GA) to properly plan the
UA V data-collecting path. Simulated trials therefore assess
the effectiveness of this approach. The experimental results
demonstrate that the hybrid method (IPSO-GA), based on
enhanced particle swarm optimization and genetic algorithms
can successfully build a path that effectively covers all data
nodes while requiring the fewest number of UA Vs.
Balasubramanian et al. 2022, the present study aims to
develop an optimal energy-efficient path planning algorithm
by combining the well-known Modified Ant Colony Opti-
mization (MACO) algorithm and a Memory-Efficient A∗
Algorithm (MEA∗) for obstacle avoidance in a three-
dimensional (3D) environment and path access optimized for
minimum power use. The proposed method combines the
two effective algorithms mentioned above to improve the
UA V path planning task. The hybrid method enables avoiding
obstacles and finding an efficient path. During the simula-
tion, the UA V attained the best trajectory during flight tests,
and the total power consumption of the UA V was com-
pared using the MACO-MEA∗algorithm which obtained
21% lower power consumption and 55% faster execution time
than the MACO-A∗technique.
Dongcheng et al. 2022 proposed study focuses on
quality-oriented UA V path planning and addresses local
dynamic hierarchical planning and global static planning.
Shorter planning timeframes and improved execution effi-
ciency are achieved by developing a three-dimensional UA V
path planning model and optimizing the A∗algorithm, search
strategy, step size, and cost function. Furthermore, the sug-
gested scheme incorporates the dynamic exploration factor
into QL to tackle the exploration-exploitation conundrum
and facilitates local dynamic path modifications. When these
methods are combined, an algorithm for global-local hybrid
UA V path planning is produced. The suggested model and
algorithm are useful for tackling path planning concerns asso-
ciated with UA Vs, such as dependability, security, and safety,
as evidenced by simulation results that show how well they
improve path quality.

A. GENETIC ALGORITHMS (GA)
Due to their capacity to operate in complex and dynamic
situations, genetic algorithms (GAs) have become increas-
ingly popular in UA V path planning optimization. For GAs
to work, a population of candidate paths represented by chro-
mosomes must be generated. These candidate paths must be
repeatedly evolved using genetic operators like mutation and
crossover [8]. GAs can search a large solution space and find
energy-efficient paths by considering a variety of prospective
paths and assessing their fitness considering predetermined
objectives.
The GA uses genetic operators like mutation and crossover
during evolution to produce new offspring by fusing and
altering the fittest members of the preceding generation [9],
[10]. Iteratively, the area covered and the energy used by each
created path are considered during the fitness evaluation. The
UA V energy consumption model considers flight direction,
altitude, and speed variables. Up until a termination criterion,
such as a maximum number of iterations or convergence
of fitness values, is met, the GA continues to evolve the
population, favouring individuals with higher fitness [10].

B. Q-LEARNING (QL) ALGORITHM
Q-learning, a type of reinforcement learning technique, has
been used to optimize UA V path planning. Reinforcement
learning enables the UA V to learn from its interactions with
its surroundings, allowing it to make intelligent decisions
based on rewards or penalties [11]. Q-learning algorithms
learn the best policy that minimizes energy consumption and
maximizes rewards depending on the UA V’s current state by
expressing the path planning problem as a Markov Decision
Process (MDP).
The following stages are utilized in the procedure for the
Q-learning algorithm in the context of UA V energy-efficient
path planning [12]. The Q-learning flowchart is depicted in
Figure 3.
■State Representation: The UA V’s current condition dur-
ing the path planning process is represented by the letter
S, which stands for the UA V’s status. The state may
contain details about the UA V’s location, speed, sepa-
ration from obstacles, and other pertinent factors [13].
■Action Representation: Define the UA V’s actions as
A, which represents the range of potential actions the
UA V may do in a specific condition. Actions that can
be taken in path planning include choosing the next
waypoint, modifying velocity, and altering altitude.
■Q-table: Keep track of a Q-table called Q (S, A) where
the learnt Q-values for each state-action pair are kept.
The Q-values show the anticipated future benefits of
performing a particular action at a particular condition.

C. HYBRID GA/QL APPROACHES
To optimize UA V path planning, hybrid approaches that mix
evolutionary algorithms and reinforcement learning methodshave shown promise. These methods build various possible
courses using genetic algorithms’ global search capabilities
and then apply reinforcement learning to adjust and refine
the paths based on current data [15]. Hybrid algorithms may
efficiently balance exploration and exploitation by combining
these two methods, which enhances energy efficiency and
adaptability.
The fitness evaluation is the foundation for a broad
framework for executing the hybrid genetic algorithm and
Q-learning approach in the UA V energy-efficient path plan-
ning context. The fitness value is the problem’s optimal
solution [16]. It uses the chromosome value with the best
fit out of all those compared at each iteration. A new chro-
mosome is formed for each cycle, along with its fitness
rating.
Learning makes it possible to assess each chromosome’s
fitness within the GA population. The cumulative rewards
earned throughout the Q-learning process can be used to
assess fitness [17]. Update the Q-values based on the
Q-learning equation using the hybrid technique afterward.
It enables to expression of the state of the UA V using per-
tinent variables such as location, velocity, and distance from
obstacles and balances the exploration of new actions with
the exploitation of obtained knowledge using an exploration-
exploitation trade-off parameter [18]. The Q-values based
on the hybrid GA-Q-learning are updated according to the
following equation.
Q (S, A) =(1−α)×Q (S, A)

where, Q (S, A) represents the Q-value for state S and

IV. METHODOLOGY AND PROPOSED SOLUTION
An energy-efficient path planning technique is provided to
develop solutions for optimal path planning with low energyusage. The algorithms utilized include GA and Q-learning,
which allow the UA V path planning to be optimized to ensure
a realistic collision-free path. We set the traveling coordinates
for UA Vs to (x i,yi) and (x j,j) respectively. The suggested
hybrid GA and Q-learning technique would allow the UA V
to identify the best path-planning solutions while travelling
through the provided coordinates. The best solution is based
on optimizing the GA fitness function by Q-learning to learn
and update the best solution results during population calcu-
lation and other fitness functions based on two criteria, the
UA V velocity and distance from the obstacles. Algorithm 1
describes the proposed energy-efficient path planning com-
putational approach.
The hybrid GA-QL algorithm begins with the population
being initialized using the Genetic Algorithm (GA). Process
in algorithm 2 evaluates the fitness of each chromosome
in the population. The GA then evolves the population by
performing selection, crossover, and mutation operations on
the offspring chromosomes, resulting in fitter offspring chro-
mosomes. The Q-table is then initialized using the fittest
chromosomes from the GA [24].
The Q-learning method is used to select the best course
of action for each state. The Q-value is updated using the
Q-learning equation, considering the reward and the max-
imum Q-value for the following state. The UA V moves to
the next state based on the action selected, and the process
is repeated until convergence or the maximum number of
iterations is reached. Throughout the process, the hybrid
technique uses a -greedy policy to balance exploration and
exploitation [25]. The state and action are updated, and the
incentive is assigned based on mission fulfilment and energy
efficiency. The stages in the flowchart are repeated until the
termination condition is reached.
The proposed algorithm depicts the processes involved
in the hybrid GA-Q-learning strategy for optimizing UA V
energy-efficient path planning. It shows how the GA and
Q-learning components collaborate to refine the solution iter-
atively and converge on an optimal path. Furthermore, when
calculating velocity and distance fitness, the dynamic scaling
factor technique is applied to improve the capacity to modify
the relative relevance or weightage given to the velocity and
distance fitness components. Algorithm 3depicts the scaling
factor approach technique.
The hybrid GA-based Q-learning optimization makes it
possible to dynamically change the relative relevance of the
velocity and distance fitness components based on the learned
Q-values by applying this dynamic scaling factor to those
components. The scaling factor rises as the Q-value rises,
emphasizing the significance of the corresponding fitness
component in total fitness assessment [26]. It is important
to keep in mind that the precise formulation and parame-
ters of the dynamic scaling factor may change based on the
problem’s demands and the Q-learning algorithm’s unique
characteristics. Experiment and adjustment may be required
to determine the best values for k that produce the desired per-
formance and balance between the fitness components [27].

D. PERFORMANCE LIMITATIONS AND POTENTIAL
IMPACTS
According to performance results, the proposed hybrid
GA-QL algorithm for UA V path planning shows good char-
acteristics. However, it may also have several limitations and
possible issues in real-world scenarios. First, in real-time
applications, the computation and temporal complexity of
the technique may cause decision delays, which may not
be acceptable for time-sensitive UA V missions. The limi-
tation lies in the requirement for specific tuning regarding
the dynamic scaling factors according to UA V missions.
Although the proposed algorithm shows that the dynamic
factors adjusting will enhance the path planning, it is required
to by intelligently optimized based on the real-time UA V
mission.

VII. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORKS
To plan UA V paths that are both energy-efficient and consider
the speed and proximity of obstacles, this study introduces
a novel hybrid genetic algorithm and Q-learning path plan-
ning optimizations approach. The suggested approach offers
a practical response to the problems associated with UA Venergy consumption while considering dynamic settings and
mission-specific limits. The experimental outcomes highlight
the hybrid approach’s potential for real-world UA V applica-
tions in diverse settings by showcasing its energy efficiency
and robustness superiority.
The innovative hybrid genetic algorithm and Q-learning
path planning optimization approach for UA V path plan-
ning that was successfully developed displayed extraordinary
capabilities in solving the dual issues of energy efficiency and
collision avoidance. Compared to GA without optimization,
our results demonstrate the outstanding performance of the
hybrid technique in reducing energy consumption by 43%
while retaining a low collision rate of 75%. Q-learning sub-
stantially contributes to the UA V path planning optimization
scenario by enabling dynamic, energy-efficient, and safe nav-
igation [57]. Its ability to learn and adapt based on previous
experiences makes it an essential component of the hybrid
genetic algorithm and Q-learning strategy, ultimately leading
to improved results in terms of reduced energy consumption
and decreased collision rates [58], [59].
Future research will concentrate on dynamic obstacle
avoidance, optimal velocity control, integrating environmen-
tal parameters, multi-UA V coordination, and real-time adapt-
ability for UA Vs. By addressing these issues, progress can
be made in developing algorithms that can handle dynamic
obstacles, optimize velocity based on energy consumption
models, adapt to changing environmental conditions, effi-
ciently coordinate multiple UA Vs, and dynamically adjust
path planning in real-time. Deep reinforcement learning
(DRL) can improve UA V path planning performance by
addressing the limitations above by autonomously learning
complex policies, making it well-suited for dynamic and
unpredictable environments. DRL will help further improve
energy efficiency and performance in UA V operations with
the ability to address complex constraints.
