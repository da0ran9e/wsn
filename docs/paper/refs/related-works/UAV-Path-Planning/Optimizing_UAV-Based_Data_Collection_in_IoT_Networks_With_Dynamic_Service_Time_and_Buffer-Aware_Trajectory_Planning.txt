Optimizing UA V-Based Data Collection in IoT
Networks With Dynamic Service Time and
Buffer-Aware Trajectory Planning
Madhu Donipati , Ankur Jaiswal ,Student Member, IEEE , Abhishek Hazra ,Member, IEEE ,
Nabajyoti Mazumdar ,Senior Member, IEEE , and Jagpreet Singh, Member, IEEE
Abstract —Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UA Vs) have become vital
tools for data collection in Internet of Things (IoT) networks,enabling efﬁcient monitoring and information acquisition acrossvarious domains. However, UA V-assisted IoT networks often facesigniﬁcant challenges such as high data loss, latency, and resourceinefﬁciency due to inadequate buffer management and dynamicservice time (DST) allocation for IoT nodes. Existing approachesfrequently overlook critical factors such as IoT nodes’ energylevels, UA Vs’ energy constraints, and the dynamic data generationrates of IoT devices. To address these challenges, this articleintroduces a novel strategy for dynamically optimizing UA V
trajectories by integrating real-time data from ground nodes and
UA V energy levels. Given the DST and buffer constraints (BC)of IoT devices, optimizing UA V trajectories for data collectionis a complex problem. We propose an optimization frameworkthat strategically plans UA V trajectories to minimize servicetime at designated Rendezvous Points (RPs) and bypasses RPswhen necessary to reduce the overall trajectory path, takinginto account buffer status and dynamic service time adjustments.To efﬁciently solve this optimization problem, we employ ameta-heuristic technique known as Path Cheapest Arc withGuided Local Search (PCA-GLS) for UA V route planning withinpredeﬁned time windows. Extensive simulations demonstratethe effectiveness of our proposed solution in optimizing UA Vtrajectories and improving data collection performance comparedto existing algorithms such as BA-UA V , ACO-MS, and NSGA-II.
Index Terms —IoT, buffer-awareness, unmanned aerial vehi-
cles, data gathering, time-windows.

A. UAV-Based Data Collection
In recent years, many studies have utilized UA Vs as mobile
data collectors for sensor nodes (SNs), extending network
lifetime [16]. Optimization of the wake-up schedule for SNs
and the UA V’s trajectory aims to minimize the maximum
energy consumption of all SNs while ensuring reliable data
collection [17]. Despite being cost-effective and ﬂexible, UA Vs
face challenges with their limited load capacity, particularly
for long-term or distant tasks. To overcome this limitation,
assembly UA Vs are proposed, focusing on optimizing energyresources through system modeling, energy cyclic cooperation,
and an in-air energy replenishing strategy [18]. UA V trajectory
planning in [6]emphasizes minimizing mission time by
optimizing UA V speed, position, and visiting sequence, con-
sidering factors like Age of Information (AoI) and on-board
energy. Methods such as successive convex approximation
(SCA) and genetic algorithms (GA) are used for speed
and path optimization, supplemented by an AoI-and-energy-aware trajectory optimization algorithm [13]. Exploration of
energy minimization in UA V-assisted sensor networks in [13]
involves optimizing trajectory and sensor power while con-sidering constraints using SCA and block coordinate descent
(BCD). This includes determining sensor serving orders and
UA V hovering positions, with the latter affecting sensorpower. Optimal sensor serving orders are determined using
the efﬁcient Cutting-Plane method for the standard traveling
salesman problem (TSP), while UA V position and sensorpower optimization employ the pattern search method for
transmit power and SCA for UA V hovering positions. In [19],
a very interesting solution for UA V energy constraints wasintroduced, suggesting a UA V data collector supported by a
mobile charging station (UGV) to minimize IoT device AoI
using multi-agent deep Q-network (MADQN) to optimizes
UA V charging, IoT servicing, and UGV trajectory, considering
obstacles and IoT movements. Strategically placing refuelingvehicles on the ground provides a promising means to extend
UA V operational range, enabling quick recharging without

IV . P ROPOSED METHODOLOGY
From the objective function, we can easily observe that
ﬁnding the shortest possible path among the RPs is complexand mostly NP-hard. Due to their computational difﬁculty, no
effective algorithms can solve NP-hard problems in polyno-
mial time. Finding optimal solutions for large instances ofNP-hard problems is computationally impossible, but several
algorithmic techniques exist to get close to the optimal
solution. Therefore, we propose a UA V-Based Data collec-tion scheme with Dynamic Service Time and Buffer-Aware
trajectory planning (DST-BA). In the proposed solution, we
have introduced heuristic algorithms: (A) Selecting RPs froma given set of nodes, (B) Planning the UA V’s Path considering
Time Windows, and (C) Dynamic Service Time Adjustment
Algorithm. The key objective is to obtain a near-optimal
solution in complex UA V communication networks.

B. UAV Path Planning With Time-Windows
The UA V path planning problem with time windows
involves ﬁnding the optimal route for a single UA V to servea number of nodes within speciﬁed time windows while
minimizing the total travel cost. This can be achieved using a
method called Path Cheapest Arc with Guided Local Search.
•Route Construction with Path Cheapest Arc:
Starting from the current node i(initially the depot), the
algorithm identiﬁes the node jthat is not yet included in the
route ( R) and has the lowest travel cost (referred as “cheapest
arc”). It ensures that visiting this node does not violate thetime window constraints. The algorithm then adds node jto
the route and proceeds to the next iteration from the newly
added node. This process continues until all nodes have beenvisited. This strategy forms the essence of the Path Cheapest
Arc algorithm.

GLS algorithm, penalties are added to certain features of the

use of high penalties can discourage the selection of certain
routes or nodes in the solution. When dealing with the UA Vrouting problem with time windows, one important aspect to
consider is the tardiness at each node, which refers to the
amount of arrival time exceeding the node’s deadline. If a
solution involves arriving late at a node, it can incur a penalty.
Initially, the algorithm may prioritize routes that result in somelate arrivals, as it allows for servicing more nodes and reducing
the total travel distance. However, when a high penalty is
applied to tardiness, the cost of such solutions increases. Thisprompts the algorithm to reject these solutions during the
local search process and instead select alternatives that meet
all deadlines, even if they require longer travel distances.This approach strikes a balance between minimizing travel
distance and meeting deadlines, leading to more practical and
reliable solutions for real-world applications. In our proposedapproach, we have incorporated a penalty for violating time
window constraints at each location.

V. D ISCUSSION OF COMPLEXITY
In this section, we discuss the computational complexity
of the proposed strategy in terms of path planning and time
adjustment in UA V-enabled data collection.1) Path Planning Complexity: The path planning phase
involves the selection of RPs and ﬁnding the optimal route forthe UA V to serve the nodes within speciﬁed time windows.
The selection of RPs is based on calculating neighborhoods
and choosing nodes with the highest number of neighbors asRPs. This step has a complexity of O(|N|), where|N| is
the number of nodes in the network. The subsequent route
construction using the Path Cheapest Arc algorithm has acomplexity of O(|N|
2), taking into account the calculation
of distances between all pairs of nodes. The complexity of
the GLS algorithm for solution improvement depends onthe speciﬁc implementation, typically ranging from O(|N|
2)
toO(|N|3). Therefore, the overall complexity of the path
planning phase can be expressed as O(|N|3).
2) Time Adjustment Complexity: The dynamic service time
adjustment algorithm considers the buffer status and dis-
tance from the depot to determine the service times at RPs.
This algorithm involves iterating through all RPs, calculat-ing distances, and adjusting service times accordingly. The
complexity of this algorithm is O(|RP|), where|RP|is the
number of RPs.
3) Overall Complexity: The proposed methodology com-
prises the path planning and time adjustment phases. Thecomplexity of the path planning phase dominates the overall
complexity. Therefore, the overall complexity of the proposed
algorithm can be approximated as O(|N|
3).

B. Network Life Time
Since IoT devices have limited energy and transmission
range, UA Vs are used to collect data by hovering at IoT nodes.This helps to conserve transmission energy across all IoT
nodes. In the event of a buffer overﬂow, all neighboring nodes
associated with the RP will be required to re-transmit theirdata to their respective RP. This process signiﬁcantly increases
energy consumption, thus reducing the lifetime of the network.
Fig. 4graphically represents how network lifetime is impacted
by an increase in the number of nodes. As the number of nodes
grows, there is a subsequent increase in neighboring nodes for
several RPs, which consequently leads to a higher incidenceof buffer overﬂows, adversely affecting the network lifetime.
The DST-BA integrates time windows and buffer awareness
for data gathering, a strategy that signiﬁcantly mitigates theprevalence of buffer overﬂows. Compared to methodologies
presented in [28] and [5], the DST-BA demonstrates lower
buffer overﬂow rates, which contributes to better energy
preservation, resulting an extension of the network lifetime.

VII. C
ONCLUSION
The proposed approach tackles the central challenge of
optimizing the UA V’s path and service time at carefullyselected RPs, leveraging buffer status and speciﬁc time win-
dows as critical factors. By implementing the PCA-GLS
method and employing the proposed innovative algorithm
for dynamic service time adjustment based on buffer status,
remarkable enhancements in the system’s overall efﬁciencyhave been witnessed. Efﬁcient planning the UA V’s route and
ensuring timely servicing of each RP within its designated
time window have maximized data collection efﬁciency withinthe IoT network. The simulation results validates that our
proposed strategy successfully optimized the UA V’s trajectory,considerably reduced service time, and improved the efﬁciency
of data collection. The proposed solution is suitable for severalapplications like, monitoring activities (forest, smart grids,
border surveillance), disaster management etc.
However, in this work, the limited and short-lived energy
sources of UA Vs signiﬁcantly constrain their operational dura-
tion. As a result, UA Vs must frequently return to the basestation (BS) to recharge their batteries, leading to substantial
penalties in terms of mission delays and disruptions. Future
work can address this issue by integrating wireless energyharvesting to recharge the onboard batteries through charging
stations or vehicles.
