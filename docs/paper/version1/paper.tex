\begin{abstract}
Urban edge-camera and IoT sensing networks increasingly support time-critical monitoring, where rapid target detection is essential in emergency scenarios. In these environments, Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) serve as mobile data ferries to disseminate high-value recognition payloads to distributed ground nodes. However, large payloads are severely constrained by short air-to-ground (A2G) contact windows and packet-level impairments such as path loss, shadowing, and stochastic packet errors (PER).

This paper addresses the problem of minimizing mission-level time-to-detection under realistic PHY-layer constraints. We propose a coordinated dissemination framework that partitions recognition payloads into small fragments of comparable information content. A UAV continuously broadcasts these fragments (rather than the full payload) along a coverage-aware trajectory, while ground nodes cooperatively exchange and fuse missing evidence through localized collaboration. Unlike conventional UAV-only delivery, our design explicitly coordinates fragment broadcasting with edge cooperation under packet-level feasibility.

Simulation results show that the proposed framework significantly reduces time-to-detection compared to monolithic transmission and UAV-only baselines, particularly under stringent A2G conditions with short contact windows and high packet error rates. The results highlight the value of jointly optimizing fine-grained dissemination, edge cooperation, and PHY-aware communication in UAV-assisted urban sensing systems.

\section{Introduction}

UAV-assisted IoT systems have emerged as a practical solution in many real-world domains. UAVs serve as flexible mobile platforms that support environmental monitoring, industrial data collection, smart agriculture, and urban intelligent systems. Thanks to their high-altitude flight and wide coverage capability, UAVs enable connectivity with spatially distributed IoT sensor nodes over large areas without relying entirely on fixed infrastructure.

The target detection task is a fundamental problem in UAV-assisted IoT systems, particularly in time-sensitive monitoring scenarios. We consider a sensing region populated by a set of ground nodes $\mathcal{N}$, where a subset of nodes $\mathcal{P} \subseteq \mathcal{N}$ is identified as candidate nodes associated with a potential event of interest. Among them, a single unknown node $n^*$ is the designated detection node that must accumulate sufficient evidence to trigger detection.

The UAV disseminates recognition data to ground nodes during flight, while nodes can further exchange received information through local cooperation. Each node incrementally builds a confidence score as it collects useful data. The objective is to enable the unknown node $n^*$ to reach a detection threshold as early as possible, under limited air-to-ground contact time and unreliable wireless links.

Traditional methods typically adopt a straightforward approach: the UAV flies close to each node and transmits the entire payload in a single session. However, this strategy requires the UAV to fly slowly or hover for extended periods to guarantee complete data delivery, which significantly increases mission duration and delays detection. Moreover, realistic physical-layer (PHY) constraints must be considered, such as short air-to-ground contact windows, path loss, shadowing, and stochastic packet errors (PER). These factors not only increase the UAV’s energy consumption but also substantially reduce overall system efficiency. Therefore, a faster and more effective approach is needed.

We propose a coordinated approach in which the UAV broadcasts compact fragments continuously along a coverage-aware path, while ground nodes perform selective edge cooperation to share missing fragments. This design helps the designated detection node accumulate evidence earlier without requiring full payload delivery to every node.

The core ideas include payload fragmentation, cell-based localized cooperation, selective fragment broadcasting along an optimized trajectory, and edge exchange of missing fragments. These components are integrated in one coordination framework to reduce detection time while keeping UAV flight cost low.

To the best of our knowledge, no prior work has jointly considered recognition payload fragmentation, coverage-aware UAV trajectory planning, and edge cooperation under realistic packet-level PHY constraints.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section~II reviews related work and positions our contribution. Section~III describes the system model. Section~IV formulates the mission-time minimization problem. Section~V presents the proposed coordination protocol in detail. Section~VI evaluates performance through extensive simulations. Section~VII discusses limitations and future extensions, and Section~VIII concludes the paper.

\section{Related Work}

%\subsection{UAV-Assisted Dissemination and AoI-Aware Scheduling}
Recent studies have widely employed UAVs as mobile platforms (data ferries) or aerial base stations to support data collection and dissemination in IoT networks \cite{wei2022uav,nguyen2021uav}. These works primarily focus on optimizing UAV trajectories to improve coverage, Age-of-Information (AoI), or detection latency \cite{liu2018age,abd2020deep}. However, most approaches assume monolithic payloads and idealized channel models (binary in-range), overlooking realistic physical-layer constraints such as short contact windows and stochastic packet error rates (PER) in urban air-to-ground (A2G) environments. Consequently, these solutions are often unsuitable for transmitting high-quality recognition payloads under harsh channel conditions.

%\subsection{Distributed Edge Detection and Cooperative Inference}
In a parallel line of research, distributed edge inference and cooperative detection aim to enhance detection accuracy through local information exchange and consensus mechanisms \cite{blum2002distributed,ren2023survey}. Recent works have also explored early-exit techniques and confidence-threshold mechanisms to reduce decision latency \cite{teerapittayanon2016branchynet}. While these approaches are effective in environments with stable infrastructure, they typically assume negligible communication overhead and continuous connectivity. In the context of UAV flyovers with extremely short contact durations, these assumptions no longer hold, rendering edge cooperation inefficient or infeasible without proper coordination with the UAV dissemination process.

%\subsection{Fragmented/Progressive Data Delivery}
A third research direction investigates progressive or fragmented data delivery, a concept long studied for bandwidth-limited channels \cite{tzou1987progressive,chee1999survey}. Recent studies have demonstrated that even when images or feature maps are fragmented, each fragment retains meaningful recognition value and can be flexibly fused using confidence accumulation, Bayesian fusion, or log-odds methods \cite{feng2019hierarchical,chen2020progressive}. In edge-AI contexts, feature-map partitioning further enables early detection without requiring the full payload \cite{mitzenmacher2004digital,zhao2018deepthings,zeng2020coedge}. Nevertheless, these techniques have largely been developed independently of UAV mobility and have not been jointly optimized with realistic A2G channel constraints at the packet level.

%\subsection{Summary and Proposed Framework}
In summary, the existing literature typically addresses the three pillars independently: UAV trajectory optimization focuses on mobility, edge cooperation emphasizes distributed inference, and fragmented delivery concentrates on data structure. 

Building upon these foundations, this paper presents a coordinated framework that integrates the three key elements---recognition payload fragmentation, coverage-aware UAV trajectory planning, and edge cooperation---under realistic packet-level PHY constraints (short contact windows and stochastic PER). Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed framework significantly reduces mission-level time-to-detection, particularly under stringent A2G channel conditions.

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}
\subsection{System Model}
\noindent\textbf{Network Model.}
We consider an urban IoT system with a base station (BS), a UAV, and a set of ground nodes $\mathcal{N}$ distributed over a monitoring region and organized into cells for localized cooperation.

A subset $\mathcal{P} \subseteq \mathcal{N}$ represents candidate nodes associated with a potential event, among which an unknown node $n^*$ must accumulate sufficient evidence for detection. The mission is completed when $n^*$ reaches a predefined confidence threshold.

Each node can receive fragments from the UAV, exchange information with neighbors, and maintain a local confidence score.

\noindent\textbf{UAV Mobility and Coverage Model.}
The UAV flies at a fixed altitude \(h\) and follows a continuous waypoint sequence \(\Pi = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_M\}\) at constant speed \(v\) without hovering. Fragments are broadcast \emph{continuously} along the entire trajectory while the UAV is in motion. Coverage at any time \(t\) is determined by the instantaneous position of the UAV and a broadcast radius \(R_b\) derived from the A2G link budget.

Let \(\mathrm{CS}(t) \subseteq \mathcal{P}\) denote the set of candidate nodes covered by the UAV at time \(t\). The trajectory is planned over a candidate set of locations (e.g., node positions or cell centroids) to maximize coverage efficiency while respecting mobility constraints.

The instantaneous Euclidean distance between the UAV and node \(n\) at time \(t\) is
\begin{equation}
d_n(t) = \sqrt{(x_{\mathrm{uav}}(t) - x_n)^2 + (y_{\mathrm{uav}}(t) - y_n)^2 + h^2}.
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Data Dissemination and Cooperation Model.}
A recognition payload is partitioned into \(K\) fragments. The UAV continuously broadcasts these fragments during flight. Let \(\mathcal{F}_n(t)\) denote the set of fragments successfully received by node \(n\) up to time \(t\).

Ground nodes engage in cooperative exchange (both intra-cell and inter-cell via designated gateways) to share received fragments and accelerate confidence accumulation at the designated detection node. The detailed triggering conditions and exchange protocols are specified in the coordination algorithms presented in Section~V.

\noindent\textbf{Fragment and Detection Model.}
Each fragment \(i\) provides an independent evidence contribution \(p_i \in (0,1)\). 
The confidence at node \(n\) accumulates over received fragments as
\begin{equation}
C_n(t) = 1 - \prod_{i \in \mathcal{F}_n(t)} (1 - p_i),
\end{equation}
which captures the union of independent evidence.

In practice, this accumulation can be implemented in a log-odds form for numerical stability and efficient incremental updates, where each fragment contributes additively to the confidence score.

\noindent\textbf{Communication and PHY Model.}
The UAV--node link follows a standard air-to-ground (A2G) channel model that accounts for distance-dependent path loss, log-normal shadowing, and small-scale fading \cite{alhourani2014modeling}. The received power at node \(n\) at time \(t\) is
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{rx}}(t) = P_{\mathrm{tx}} - \mathrm{PL}(d_n(t)) - X_{\sigma}(t) - X_f(t),
\end{equation}
where \(P_{\mathrm{tx}}\) is the transmit power, \(\mathrm{PL}(\cdot)\) is the path-loss function, \(X_{\sigma}(t)\) is log-normal shadowing, and \(X_f(t)\) is small-scale fading.

The instantaneous signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) determines the bit error rate (BER). This BER is mapped to packet error rate (PER) via
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PER} = 1 - (1 - \mathrm{BER})^{8L},
\end{equation}
where \(L\) is the packet size in bytes \cite{alhourani2014modeling}. A packet is successfully received only if (i) \(P_{\mathrm{rx}}(t) \ge P_{\mathrm{sens}}\) (receiver sensitivity threshold) and (ii) the condition holds throughout the entire packet transmission duration.

\noindent\textbf{Timing and Contact Model.}
Let \(t_{\mathrm{tx}} = 8L / R\) denote the transmission time of one packet, where \(R\) is the data rate. Because the UAV broadcasts continuously while moving, a \emph{contact window} between the UAV and node \(n\) is defined as the maximal continuous time interval during which \(P_{\mathrm{rx}}(t) \ge P_{\mathrm{sens}}\). All fragment deliveries and cooperative exchanges must complete within these valid contact windows. Scheduling decisions therefore incorporate both instantaneous channel feasibility and per-node buffer constraints.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Let \(T_{\mathrm{detect}}\) be the earliest time at which the designated detection node \(n^*\) reaches the alert threshold:
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{detect}} = \inf \{ t \mid C_{n^*}(t) \ge \tau_{\mathrm{alert}} \}.
\end{equation}

The objective is to minimize the mission-level detection time:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\Pi,\, \mathcal{S}} \quad T_{\mathrm{detect}},
\end{equation}
where \(\Pi\) is the UAV waypoint sequence and \(\mathcal{S}\) is the joint scheduling policy for fragment broadcasting and cooperative exchange. The optimization is subject to UAV mobility constraints (speed and trajectory continuity), communication constraints (contact windows, PER, and receiver sensitivity), intra- and inter-cell cooperation limits, and per-node buffer/scheduling constraints.
\section{Proposed Approach}

\noindent\textbf{Overview.}
The proposed coordinated dissemination framework integrates three tightly coupled modules that operate in a closed loop: (i) coverage-aware UAV trajectory planning, (ii) continuous fragment broadcasting, and (iii) distributed edge cooperation. The base station (BS) first constructs a globally near-optimal UAV path using a greedy maximum-coverage (GMC) heuristic. While the UAV flies along this path, it continuously broadcasts recognition fragments. Ground nodes update their local confidence scores upon successful reception and trigger cooperative exchanges when necessary to accelerate evidence accumulation at the designated detection node \(n^*\).

The framework takes as input the network topology, candidate-node set \(\mathcal{P}\), threshold parameters \(\tau_{\text{coop}}\) and \(\tau_{\text{alert}}\), and PHY settings. It outputs the UAV waypoint sequence \(\Pi\), the transmission schedule \(\mathcal{S}\), and the detection time \(T_{\text{detect}}\). A heuristic design is adopted because the joint optimization of , scheduling, and random packet outcomes is computationally intractable for real-time missions.

\noindent\textbf{Cell Formation and Local Coordination Structure.}

To support efficient localized cooperation under limited communication range, ground nodes are organized into geographic cells. This cell structure reduces coordination overhead, limits contention, and enables scalable fragment exchange within bounded neighborhoods.

Fig.~\ref{fig:cell_and_tree} illustrates the cell partitioning, leader election outcome, and gateway-to-gateway inter-cell forwarding links used by the coordination protocol.

The cell formation and leader election procedure is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:cell}. Nodes are grouped based on spatial proximity, and a lightweight distributed selection mechanism is used to assign coordination roles within each cell.

\noindent\textbf{UAV Trajectory Planning (GMC).}
To achieve efficient spatial coverage with minimal flight distance, we employ a greedy maximum-coverage (GMC) heuristic. Candidate waypoints are selected from node positions and cell centroids. The algorithm iteratively chooses the waypoint that provides the highest coverage gain per unit travel cost while considering cell-level coverage (if a waypoint covers more than a threshold fraction \(\beta\) of a cell, the entire cell is marked as covered). The resulting trajectory \(\Pi\) balances coverage efficiency and travel cost.

The pseudocode of the GMC planner is given in Algorithm~\ref{alg:gmc}.

\noindent\textbf{Fragment Broadcasting and Confidence Update.}
Along the planned trajectory \(\Pi\), the UAV continuously broadcasts the \(K\) fragments in a cyclic manner without hovering. Each ground node \(n\) maintains a local set of received fragments \(\mathcal{F}_n(t)\) and updates its confidence score \(C_n(t)\) incrementally whenever a new fragment is successfully received (using either the union-probability or log-odds form presented in Section~III).

\noindent\textbf{Cooperative Edge Protocol.}
To compensate for packet losses caused by short contact windows, nodes engage in both intra-cell and inter-cell cooperation. Cooperation is triggered by three events: (i) local confidence exceeding \(\tau_{\text{coop}}\), (ii) the end of a UAV contact window, or (iii) receipt of a manifest revealing missing fragments. Upon triggering, a node broadcasts a compact manifest and exchanges missing fragments with peers. Cell gateways handle inter-cell forwarding when necessary, using timeout and jitter mechanisms to avoid collisions.

The complete cooperative exchange logic is detailed in Algorithm~\ref{alg:coop}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Coordinated Detection Workflow (Simplified)}
\label{alg:workflow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\mathcal{G}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{D}$, PHY params, $n^*$
\Ensure $\Pi, \mathcal{S}, T_{\text{detect}}$

\State Initialize cells $\{\mathcal{C}_i\}$, leaders, and coverage map
\State Partition $\mathcal{D}$ into $K$ fragments
\State Compute UAV path $\Pi$ via GMC
\State Initialize $C_n \gets 0, \forall n$

\For{each waypoint $w \in \Pi$}
    \State UAV broadcasts fragments
    \For{each node $n$ receiving new fragments}
        \State Update $C_n$

        \If{$C_n \ge \tau_{\text{coop}}$}
            \State Trigger cooperative exchange
        \EndIf

        \If{$n = n^*$ and $C_n \ge \tau_{\text{alert}}$}
            \State \Return $T_{\text{detect}}$
        \EndIf
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\caption{Cell Formation and Leader Election}
\label{alg:cell}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Node set $\mathcal{N}$ with positions
\Ensure Cell structure $\{\mathcal{C}_i\}$ with leaders

\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Coverage-Aware UAV Path Planning (GMC)}
\label{alg:gmc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Candidate set $\mathcal{C}$, candidate-node set $\mathcal{P}$
\Ensure Ordered waypoint list $\Pi$

\State Initialize $\Pi \gets \emptyset$, $\mathcal{P}_{\text{covered}} \gets \emptyset$

\While{$\mathcal{P}_{\text{covered}} \neq \mathcal{P}$}
    \For{each candidate $c \in \mathcal{C}$}
        \State Compute coverage gain $G(c)$ over uncovered nodes
        \If{$c$ covers $> \beta$ fraction of a cell}
            \State Mark entire cell as covered
        \EndIf
        \State Compute cost $L(c)$ (travel distance)
        \State Score $S(c) \gets \frac{G(c)}{L(c)^\alpha + \epsilon}$
    \EndFor

    \State Select $c^* = \arg\max S(c)$
    \State Append $c^*$ to $\Pi$
    \State Update $\mathcal{P}_{\text{covered}}$
\EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Cooperative Exchange (Intra- and Inter-cell)}
\label{alg:coop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Node $n$, fragment set $\mathcal{F}_n$, thresholds $\tau_{\text{coop}},\tau_{\text{alert}}$, cell gateway (CGW), neighbor-cell gateway (NGW)
\Ensure Updated fragment sets and confidence

\State Trigger := ( $C_n \ge \tau_{\text{coop}}$ ) 
       OR (UAV contact ended) 
       OR (peer manifest reveals missing fragments)

\If{Trigger}
    \State Broadcast manifest; receive requests; exchange missing fragments with neighbors (with collision-aware scheduling)
    \State Update $\mathcal{F}_n$ and $C_n$
\EndIf

\If{cell gateway (CGW) detects stagnation (no new manifests) AND $\exists n: C_n < \tau_{\text{coop}}$}
    \If{neighbor-cell gateway (NGW) has missing fragments AND target nodes satisfy $C_n < \tau_{\text{alert}}$ (after timeout)}
        \State Request and forward fragments via gateway-to-gateway coordination
        \State Update fragment sets and confidences across cells
    \EndIf
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Metrics}
To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed fragment-based cooperative detection and GMC planning, we employ the following metrics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Detection Time ($T_{\text{detect}}$)}: The primary metric, defined as the time interval from UAV flight initialization until the designated detection node reaches the alert threshold $\tau_{\text{alert}}$.
    \item \textbf{UAV Path Length}: The cumulative distance traveled by the UAV along its waypoint sequence until the mission is completed.
    \item \textbf{Cooperation Gain}: The ratio of fragments received through intra-cell cooperative exchange versus those received directly from UAV broadcasts. This measures the effectiveness of the cell layer in compensating for link failures.
\end{itemize}

Fig.~\ref{fig:uav-path-three} compares UAV trajectories under different strategies. Coverage-aware (GMC-based) planning produces shorter and more structured paths than NN. The proposed cell-aware variant further reduces travel distance while maintaining effective coverage, improving overall movement efficiency. The designated detection node can accumulate useful information from neighboring nodes even before direct UAV contact, and a substantial portion of the final confidence is contributed by shared fragments. This demonstrates the role of cooperation in amplifying information, particularly in larger networks.

Overall, the results indicate a shift in design strategy: instead of attempting full payload delivery over short and unreliable A2G links, continuous fragment dissemination combined with local cooperation enables steady and efficient information accumulation. These findings support the central premise of this work that jointly coordinating dissemination, trajectory planning, and edge cooperation is key to reducing detection latency under realistic A2G constraints.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper studied the problem of minimizing mission-level time-to-detection in UAV-assisted urban IoT sensing systems under realistic packet-level communication constraints. We proposed a coordinated framework that integrates three key mechanisms: payload fragmentation, coverage-aware UAV trajectory planning, and localized edge cooperation. By continuously broadcasting fragments during flight and enabling nodes to exchange missing information, the system accelerates confidence accumulation at the designated detection node without requiring full payload delivery.

The design is built on a realistic A2G communication model incorporating path loss, shadowing, fading, receiver sensitivity, and packet errors, making it suitable for harsh urban environments with short contact windows. Simulation results indicate that the proposed approach reduces detection latency and UAV travel cost compared with conventional node-by-node delivery, while demonstrating the effectiveness of edge cooperation in mitigating unreliable links.

Overall, the results highlight that jointly designing dissemination, mobility, and cooperation is critical for fast target detection in UAV-assisted IoT systems.

Future work includes adaptive trajectory design under dynamic channel conditions, more efficient cooperation control, multi-UAV extensions, and integration with task-oriented fragment generation from real edge-AI models.
