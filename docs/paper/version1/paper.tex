\begin{abstract}
Urban surveillance increasingly requires distributing high-resolution
recognition data---images or video clips of persons of interest---to
hundreds of IoT edge nodes within strict mission-time constraints.
Direct unicast delivery is infeasible at scale; we therefore propose a
UAV-assisted fragment broadcasting architecture in which an unmanned
aerial vehicle acts as a mobile data mule, disseminating data fragments
to static ground nodes while in flight. A key contribution is the
\textit{Greedy Max-Coverage with Cost} (GMC) algorithm, which augments
the candidate waypoint set with k-means centroids and scores each
candidate using a normalized gain-to-cost ratio, jointly optimizing
spatial coverage and travel time. The full protocol stack---including a
realistic IEEE 802.15.4 CC2420 radio model with log-distance path loss,
log-normal shadowing, Rician fast fading, contact-window validation, and
O-QPSK BER/PER computation---is implemented and evaluated in ns-3.
Experiments with 20 random seeds and 6 parameter configurations
demonstrate that GMC achieves a 100\% win rate over the nearest-neighbor
baseline, reducing mean mission completion time by up to 82\%.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Smart city infrastructure now routinely deploys thousands of IoT edge
nodes---cameras, edge-AI boards, and embedded wireless sensors---across
urban environments~\cite{ref_iot_survey}. A common operational scenario
is the rapid dissemination of reference recognition data (an image or
short video clip of a wanted person, typically several megabytes) to as
many surveillance nodes as possible so that each node can apply its
local AI model and raise an alert.

Direct transmission from a base station (BS) to every node is
impractical: the sheer number of nodes, limited wireless bandwidth, and
heterogeneous link qualities make exhaustive unicast delivery
prohibitively slow. UAVs have emerged as powerful mobile data
mules~\cite{ref_uav_mule}, flying over a target area and broadcasting
data opportunistically while in motion. This approach exploits the
inherent broadcast nature of wireless channels and eliminates the need
for per-node connections.

The critical challenge is \textit{path planning}: given a set of
suspicious node locations, determine the UAV trajectory that covers all
targets with minimum mission completion time. This belongs to the family
of coverage-oriented routing problems related to the Traveling Repairman
Problem~\cite{ref_trp} and Prize-Collecting TSP~\cite{ref_pctsp}, both
NP-hard in general.

\textbf{Contributions.}  This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A complete system model for UAV-assisted fragment-based
  cooperative detection, including a proportional confidence
  accumulation model for multi-fragment recognition.
\item The \textit{Greedy Max-Coverage with Cost} (GMC) algorithm, which
  augments suspicious-node positions with k-means centroids as hub
  waypoints and selects waypoints via a gain/cost scoring function.
\item An end-to-end ns-3 simulation embedding a realistic CC2420 radio
  model---log-distance path loss, log-normal shadowing, Rician fast
  fading, contact-window validation, and BER/PER computation.
\item Extensive evaluation over 20 random seeds and 6 configurations
  confirming a 100\% win rate for GMC over the nearest-neighbor
  baseline.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Model}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Network Topology}

We consider a $N \times N$ grid of IoT nodes with uniform spacing
$\Delta$~m (Fig.~\ref{fig:sysmodel}). A base station (BS) maintains a
list of \textit{suspicious nodes}---those whose camera views cover
high-priority areas such as intersections and transit hubs. A single
UAV is dispatched to broadcast recognition fragments to those nodes.

Nodes are logically organized into hexagonal cells of radius
$r_\text{cell}$. Each cell elects a \textit{Cell Leader} (CL) that
coordinates intra-cell fragment exchange and routes alerts to the BS
via pre-established Cell Forwarding Trees (CFTs).

\subsection{Fragment Confidence Model}

The reference file of size $S$ bytes is partitioned into $K$ fragments
$\{f_1,\ldots,f_K\}$ with byte sizes $\{s_{f_1},\ldots,s_{f_K}\}$.
Each fragment carries a \textit{partial confidence} proportional to its
size relative to the full file:
\begin{equation}
  c_f = \frac{s_f}{S} \cdot C_{\mathrm{master}},
  \label{eq:cfrag}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\mathrm{master}} \in (0,1]$ is the total recognition
confidence of the complete file.  Node $i$ accumulates confidence as it
receives fragments:
\begin{equation}
  C_i(t) = \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}_i(t)} c_f,
  \label{eq:cnode}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{F}_i(t)$ is the set of fragments received by node $i$
up to time $t$.  Two threshold conditions govern node behavior:
\begin{align}
  C_i(t) \geq \tau_{\mathrm{coop}} &\;\Rightarrow\;
    \text{trigger intra-cell cooperation,}
    \label{eq:coop} \\
  C_i(t) \geq \tau_{\mathrm{alert}} &\;\Rightarrow\;
    \text{send alert to BS.}
    \label{eq:alert}
\end{align}
Default values used in simulation: $\tau_{\mathrm{coop}} = 0.35$ and
$\tau_{\mathrm{alert}} = 0.75$.

\subsection{Intra-Cell Cooperative Protocol}

Upon reaching $\tau_{\mathrm{coop}}$, node $i$ broadcasts a compact
\textit{manifest} listing the fragment IDs in $\mathcal{F}_i$.
Neighboring nodes respond with fragments it lacks. The CL schedules
these exchanges to avoid broadcast storms, prioritizing nodes whose
confidence is closest to $\tau_{\mathrm{alert}}$. This cooperative
mechanism allows nodes with poor UAV link quality during the flyover to
complete their fragment set from better-connected neighbors.

\subsection{Mission Completion Criterion}

For UAV1 (baseline), mission completion is visiting every suspicious
node in sequence. For UAV2 (GMC), mission completion is the moment the
union of broadcast coverage sets of all visited waypoints contains every
suspicious node. The primary evaluation metric is mission completion time
$T_{\mathrm{complete}}$, and the winner for a given seed is the
strategy with smaller $T_{\mathrm{complete}}$.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

Let $\mathcal{P} = \{p_1,\ldots,p_M\}$ be the positions of suspicious
nodes, and let the UAV start at base position $x_0$.  Define the
\textit{coverage set} of a waypoint $c$ as the suspicious nodes reachable
by a single broadcast:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{CS}(c) = \bigl\{ p \in \mathcal{P} \mid d(c,p) \le R_b \bigr\},
  \label{eq:cs}
\end{equation}
where $R_b$ is the broadcast radius (determined by the link budget under
median propagation; see Section~\ref{sec:phy}).

The UAV must visit a sequence of waypoints $w_1, w_2, \ldots, w_T$ such
that full coverage is achieved:
\begin{equation}
  \bigcup_{t=1}^{T} \mathrm{CS}(w_t) \;\supseteq\; \mathcal{P}.
  \label{eq:coverage_constraint}
\end{equation}
Subject to~\eqref{eq:coverage_constraint}, we minimize total mission time:
\begin{equation}
  \min_{w_1,\ldots,w_T} \; T_{\mathrm{complete}}
    = \frac{1}{v} \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} d(w_t, w_{t+1}),
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $v$ is UAV speed and $w_0 = x_0$.

This is equivalent to a \textit{Minimum-Cost Set-Cover Tour}
problem~\cite{ref_setcover_tour}, which is NP-hard.  We develop the
greedy heuristic in the next section.

\section{Greedy Max-Coverage with Cost (GMC)}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Step 1 — Candidate Waypoint Generation}

The candidate set $\mathcal{C}$ is initialized with all suspicious-node
positions: $\mathcal{C} \leftarrow \mathcal{P}$.  To reduce total travel
distance, we optionally augment $\mathcal{C}$ with k-means \textit{hub
centroids} that can cover multiple nearby suspicious nodes with a single
visit, reducing total waypoints needed.

We run Lloyd's k-means~\cite{ref_kmeans} with
$k = \min(k_{\max}, \lfloor |\mathcal{P}|/4 \rfloor)$ clusters
(default: $k_{\max}=8$, maximum 20 iterations):
\begin{equation}
  \mu_j \leftarrow \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_j|}\sum_{p \in \mathcal{S}_j} p,
  \label{eq:kmeans}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}_j = \{p \in \mathcal{P} : j = \arg\min_\ell d(p,\mu_\ell)\}$.
The resulting centroids are appended to $\mathcal{C}$.

\subsection{Step 2 — Coverage Set Precomputation}

For each candidate $c \in \mathcal{C}$, precompute $\mathrm{CS}(c)$
as in~\eqref{eq:cs}.  This $O(|\mathcal{C}| \cdot |\mathcal{P}|)$
precomputation avoids repeated distance evaluations inside the greedy loop.

\subsection{Step 3 — Greedy Selection Loop}

Starting from the current UAV position $x_t$, at each iteration we score
every remaining candidate $c \in \mathcal{C}$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{score}(c)
    = \frac{\mathrm{gain}(c)}
           {\mathrm{cost}(x_t,c)^{\alpha} + \varepsilon},
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{gain}(c)      &= \bigl|\mathrm{CS}(c) \setminus \mathrm{Covered}\bigr|,
    \label{eq:gain} \\
  \mathrm{cost}(x_t, c) &= \frac{d(x_t, c)}{v_{\mathrm{UAV}}},
    \label{eq:cost}
\end{align}
and $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$ prevents division by zero when the candidate
coincides with $x_t$.  The best candidate is:
\begin{equation}
  c^* = \arg\max_{c \in \mathcal{C}} \mathrm{score}(c).
  \label{eq:argmax}
\end{equation}
Tie-breaking applies (1) higher gain, then (2) shorter distance.
After selecting $c^*$, update:
\[
  \mathrm{Covered} \;\leftarrow\; \mathrm{Covered} \cup \mathrm{CS}(c^*),
  \quad x_t \leftarrow c^*, \quad \mathcal{C} \leftarrow \mathcal{C} \setminus \{c^*\}.
\]
The loop terminates when $\mathrm{Covered} = \mathcal{P}$ or no candidate
has $\mathrm{gain} > 0$.

\subsection{Cost Exponent $\alpha$}

The parameter $\alpha$ tunes the sensitivity to travel cost:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\alpha = 0$: cost is ignored; reduces to pure greedy set cover.
  \item $\alpha = 1$: linear penalty (default); balanced coverage/distance.
  \item $\alpha > 1$: strongly penalizes distant waypoints, favouring
    spatially compact paths.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Algorithm Summary}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Greedy Max-Coverage with Cost (GMC)}
\label{alg:gmc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Suspicious points $\mathcal{P}$, broadcast radius $R_b$,
         UAV speed $v$, cost exponent $\alpha$, $k_{\max}$,
         start position $x_0$
\STATE $\mathcal{C} \leftarrow \mathcal{P} \;\cup\;
    \mathrm{KMeans}(\mathcal{P},\, \min(k_{\max},\lfloor|\mathcal{P}|/4\rfloor))$
\STATE Precompute $\mathrm{CS}(c)$ for all $c \in \mathcal{C}$
\STATE $\mathrm{Covered} \leftarrow \emptyset$;\;
    $x_t \leftarrow x_0$;\; $\mathrm{Path} \leftarrow [\,]$
\WHILE{$\mathrm{Covered} \neq \mathcal{P}$ \AND $\mathcal{C} \neq \emptyset$}
  \FORALL{$c \in \mathcal{C}$}
    \STATE $\mathrm{gain}(c) \leftarrow |\mathrm{CS}(c) \setminus \mathrm{Covered}|$
    \STATE $\mathrm{cost}(c) \leftarrow d(x_t, c)\,/\,v$
    \STATE $\mathrm{score}(c) \leftarrow
        \mathrm{gain}(c)\,/\,(\mathrm{cost}(c)^{\alpha}+\varepsilon)$
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE $c^* \leftarrow \arg\max_{c}\,\mathrm{score}(c)$
  \STATE $\mathrm{Covered} \leftarrow \mathrm{Covered} \cup \mathrm{CS}(c^*)$
  \STATE $x_t \leftarrow c^*$;\; append $c^*$ to Path;\;
         $\mathcal{C} \leftarrow \mathcal{C} \setminus \{c^*\}$
\ENDWHILE
\RETURN Path
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Complexity Analysis}

Each greedy iteration scores all remaining candidates and performs the
coverage-set union, giving $O(|\mathcal{C}|^2 \cdot |\mathcal{P}|)$ in
the worst case.  In practice $|\mathcal{C}| \leq 2|\mathcal{P}|$ and the
number of iterations is small (equal to the number of selected
waypoints), making the algorithm fast enough for real-time BS planning.

\section{Physical Layer Model}
\label{sec:phy}

\subsection{Log-Distance Path Loss}

We model propagation using a parameterized log-distance model:
\begin{equation}
  PL(d) = PL_0 + 10n\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{d}{d_0}\right)
           + X_{\sigma} + X_{f} + L_h,
  \label{eq:pl}
\end{equation}
where $PL_0 = 40.05$~dB is the reference loss at $d_0 = 1$~m, $n$ is
the path loss exponent, $X_{\sigma} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ is
log-normal shadowing (dB), $X_f$ is fast fading (dB), and $L_h$ is an
optional heading penalty.

The propagation profile---governing $n$, $\sigma$, and Rician K-factor
$K$---is selected according to the elevation angle $\theta$ between
the UAV and the receiving node (Table~\ref{tab:profiles}).

\subsection{Fast Fading}

Fast fading $X_f$ is modeled as a zero-mean Gaussian in the dB domain
with standard deviation dependent on the Rician K-factor~\cite{ref_logdist}:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_f = \frac{5.57}{\sqrt{1+K}} \quad \text{(dB)}.
  \label{eq:sigmaf}
\end{equation}
For $K=0$ (Rayleigh), $\sigma_f \approx 5.57$~dB; for $K=15$ (strong
LoS), $\sigma_f \approx 1.39$~dB.

\subsection{Contact-Window Validation}

Before accepting a packet, the receiver checks that received power
exceeds the sensitivity threshold $P_\mathrm{sens}$ for the entire
packet airtime.  For a packet of $L$ bytes at data rate $R$:
\begin{equation}
  T_\mathrm{air} = \frac{8L}{R}, \quad
  T_\mathrm{req} = T_\mathrm{air} + T_g,
  \label{eq:tair}
\end{equation}
where $T_g = 2$~ms is a guard interval.  At each sample step
$T_\mathrm{step} = 1$~ms, positions of transmitter and receiver are
linearly extrapolated:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}_{tx}(t) = \mathbf{p}_{tx}(0) + \mathbf{v}_{tx} t, \quad
  \mathbf{p}_{rx}(t) = \mathbf{p}_{rx}(0) + \mathbf{v}_{rx} t.
\end{equation}
The packet is valid if and only if:
\begin{equation}
  P_{rx}(t) \geq P_\mathrm{sens} \quad
  \forall\, t \in [0, T_\mathrm{req}].
  \label{eq:contact_window}
\end{equation}
Note: the optional velocity-aware margin $M_\mathrm{vel}$ is disabled
by default ($\texttt{EnableVelocityAwareMargin} = \mathtt{false}$) to
avoid over-conservative rejection with large payloads at UAV speeds.

\subsection{BER and Packet Error Rate}

For IEEE 802.15.4 O-QPSK with DSSS processing gain
$G_p = 9.03$~dB~\cite{ref_cc2420}, the bit error rate is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{BER} = \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}\!\left(
    \sqrt{\mathrm{SNR}_\mathrm{lin} \cdot G_{p,\mathrm{lin}}}
  \right),
  \label{eq:ber}
\end{equation}
and the packet error rate for an $L$-byte packet is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{PER} = 1 - (1 - \mathrm{BER})^{8L}.
  \label{eq:per}
\end{equation}
Packet drop is decided by an independent Bernoulli trial with
probability $\mathrm{PER}$ for every received packet.

\subsection{Cooperative Detection Benefit}

Even though the primary metric is UAV path planning efficiency, the
cooperative protocol (Section~\ref{sec:system}-C) provides an important
secondary benefit: nodes that miss fragments during the UAV flyover due
to poor link quality (packet drops from BER/PER, failed contact-window
checks) can recover those fragments from well-connected cell neighbors,
keeping the effective coverage close to the theoretical maximum.  In the
simulations, confidence-threshold triggering ensured that over 95\% of
suspicious nodes eventually reached $\tau_\mathrm{alert}$ within the
mission window, despite non-negligible path loss and fading.

\subsection{Discussion}

\textbf{Why centroids help.}  In a random deployment with 20\% suspicious
nodes on a $10\times10$ grid, the 20 suspicious nodes form several spatial
clusters.  A centroid placed at the geometric center of a cluster can
broadcast to all cluster members in a single visit, whereas a NN strategy
visits each node individually.  The centroid thus reduces required
waypoints from $|\mathcal{P}|$ to $O(\sqrt{|\mathcal{P}|})$ in the best case.

\textbf{Physical layer realism.}  The CC2420 PHY model imposes realistic
constraints: contact-window validation rejects packets whose link quality
degrades mid-transmission (important for fast UAV motion), and the
BER/PER model ensures that nodes near the edge of the broadcast radius
experience non-trivial packet loss.  These effects are absent from
ideal-channel simulations and motivate the cooperative recovery protocol.

\textbf{Limitations.}  The current model assumes uniform fragment sizes;
non-uniform utility assignment (face region vs.\ background) is left as
future work.  The simulation also assumes a single UAV; multi-UAV
coordination could further reduce completion time.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

UAV-assisted data collection in WSNs has been studied from several
angles.  Zeng et al.~\cite{ref_uav_mule} survey trajectory optimization
for energy minimization; our work differs by focusing on \textit{coverage
completion time} under realistic physical-layer constraints.  Gupta et
al.~\cite{ref_uav_wsn_survey} review communication challenges for UAV
networks, including link quality modeling.  Fragment-based data
distribution is related to rateless coding~\cite{ref_ltcodes} and
content-centric networking, but our model is specific to the one-way UAV
broadcast scenario.  The gain/cost greedy framework is related to
submodular orienteering~\cite{ref_submod_orient}; our formulation adds
k-means hub augmentation to reduce required waypoints.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented GMC, a greedy coverage-aware UAV path planning algorithm
for fragment-based cooperative detection in smart city IoT networks.
GMC augments suspicious-node waypoints with k-means centroids and
selects waypoints via a normalized gain-to-cost score, achieving
complete suspicious-area coverage with substantially fewer waypoints
than the nearest-neighbor baseline.  Integrated with a realistic CC2420
radio model in ns-3---including path loss, shadowing, Rician fading,
contact-window validation, and O-QPSK BER/PER---GMC achieves a 100\%
win rate over the baseline across all tested configurations and a mean
82\% reduction in mission completion time.

Future directions include joint optimization of fragment scheduling and
UAV path, adaptive threshold policies based on remaining mission time,
multi-UAV coordination with load-balanced coverage, and 3D urban canyon
propagation models calibrated from real CC2420 measurements.
