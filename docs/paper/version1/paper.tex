\begin{abstract}
Urban edge-camera and IoT sensing networks are widely deployed for time-critical monitoring, where rapid target detection is critical in emergency scenarios. In these environments, Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) serve as mobile data ferries to disseminate high-value recognition payloads to distributed ground nodes. However, large payloads are severely constrained by short air-to-ground (A2G) contact windows and packet-level impairments such as path loss, shadowing, and stochastic packet errors (PER).

This paper addresses the problem of minimizing mission-level time-to-detection under realistic PHY-layer constraints. We propose a coordinated dissemination framework that partitions recognition payloads into small fragments of comparable information content. A UAV continuously broadcasts these fragments along a coverage-aware trajectory, while ground nodes cooperatively exchange and fuse missing fragments through localized collaboration. Unlike conventional UAV-only delivery, our design explicitly coordinates fragment broadcasting with edge cooperation under packet-level feasibility.

Simulation results show that the proposed framework significantly reduces time-to-detection compared to monolithic transmission and UAV-only baselines, particularly under stringent A2G conditions with short contact windows and high packet error rates. The results highlight the value of jointly optimizing fine-grained dissemination, edge cooperation, and PHY-aware communication in UAV-assisted urban sensing systems.

\section{Introduction}

In recent years, UAV-assisted IoT applications have developed rapidly and become an important solution in many real-world domains. UAVs serve as flexible mobile platforms that support environmental monitoring, industrial data collection, smart agriculture, and urban intelligent systems. Thanks to their high-altitude flight and wide coverage capability, UAVs enable connectivity with spatially distributed IoT sensor nodes over large areas without relying entirely on fixed infrastructure.

The target detection task is a fundamental problem in UAV-assisted IoT systems, particularly in time-sensitive monitoring scenarios. We consider a sensing region populated by a set of ground nodes $\mathcal{N}$, where a subset of nodes $\mathcal{P} \subseteq \mathcal{N}$ is identified as candidate locations associated with a potential event of interest. Among them, a single unknown node $n^*$ is the one that must accumulate sufficient evidence to trigger detection.

The UAV disseminates recognition data to ground nodes during flight, while nodes can further exchange received information through local cooperation. Each node incrementally builds a confidence score as it collects useful data. The objective is to enable the unknown node $n^*$ to reach a detection threshold as early as possible, under limited air-to-ground contact time and unreliable wireless links.

Traditional methods typically adopt a straightforward approach: the UAV flies close to each node and transmits the entire payload in a single session. However, this strategy requires the UAV to fly slowly or hover for extended periods to guarantee complete data delivery, resulting in significantly prolonged mission completion time. Moreover, realistic physical-layer (PHY) constraints must be considered, such as short air-to-ground contact windows, path loss, shadowing, and stochastic packet errors (PER). These factors not only increase the UAV’s energy consumption but also substantially reduce overall system efficiency. Therefore, a faster and more effective approach is needed.

We propose a new comprehensive coordinated approach. Instead of transmitting a large monolithic payload, the UAV disseminates only a small number of fragments, each carrying comparable recognition value. These fragments are broadcast continuously while the UAV is in motion, combined with an efficient trajectory strategy that maximizes coverage of the target area without unnecessary overlaps. At the same time, the ground IoT nodes collaborate closely through edge fusion and selective fragment exchange. This enables the suspicion node to accumulate sufficient detection evidence much earlier without requiring the UAV to deliver the complete payload to every individual node.

This approach is built upon a clear understanding of the air-to-ground (A2G) channel and realistic PHY constraints. Specifically, the system accounts for UAV-node distance, distance-dependent path loss, shadowing, and fading effects when deciding the timing and manner of fragment transmission, ensuring feasibility at the packet level under harsh urban channel conditions.

The core ideas of the proposed solution include payload fragmentation, organization of nodes into cell layers to support localized cooperation, selective fragment broadcasting along an optimized trajectory, and an edge cooperation mechanism that allows nodes to efficiently exchange missing fragments. These components are tightly integrated within a single coordination framework, significantly reducing target detection time while keeping the UAV’s flight path cost low.

To the best of our knowledge, the solution proposed in this paper is the first work that simultaneously integrates three key elements: recognition payload fragmentation, coverage-aware UAV trajectory planning, and edge cooperation under realistic packet-level PHY constraints.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section~II reviews related work and identifies the research gap. Section~III describes the system model. Section~IV formulates the mission-time minimization problem. Section~V presents the proposed coordination protocol in detail. Section~VI evaluates performance through extensive simulations. Section~VII discusses limitations and future extensions, and Section~VIII concludes the paper.

\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textbf{UAV-Assisted Dissemination and AoI-Aware Scheduling}
Recent studies have widely employed UAVs as mobile platforms (data ferries) or aerial base stations to support data collection and dissemination in IoT networks \cite{wei2022uav,nguyen2021uav}. These works primarily focus on optimizing UAV trajectories to improve coverage, Age-of-Information (AoI), or mission completion time \cite{liu2018age,abd2020deep}. However, most approaches assume monolithic payloads and idealized channel models (binary in-range), overlooking realistic physical-layer constraints such as short contact windows and stochastic packet error rates (PER) in urban air-to-ground (A2G) environments. Consequently, these solutions are often unsuitable for transmitting high-quality recognition payloads under harsh channel conditions.

\noindent\textbf{Distributed Edge Detection and Cooperative Inference}
In a parallel line of research, distributed edge inference and cooperative detection aim to enhance detection accuracy through local information exchange and consensus mechanisms \cite{blum2002distributed,ren2023survey}. Recent works have also explored early-exit techniques and confidence-threshold mechanisms to reduce decision latency \cite{teerapittayanon2016branchynet}. While these approaches are effective in environments with stable infrastructure, they typically assume negligible communication overhead and continuous connectivity. In the context of UAV flyovers with extremely short contact durations, these assumptions no longer hold, rendering edge cooperation inefficient or infeasible without proper coordination with the UAV dissemination process.

\noindent\textbf{Fragmented/Progressive Data Delivery}
A third research direction investigates progressive or fragmented data delivery, a concept long studied for bandwidth-limited channels \cite{tzou1987progressive,chee1999survey}. Recent studies have demonstrated that even when images or feature maps are fragmented, each fragment retains meaningful recognition value and can be flexibly fused using confidence accumulation, Bayesian fusion, or log-odds methods \cite{feng2019hierarchical,chen2020progressive}. In edge-AI contexts, feature-map partitioning further enables early detection without requiring the full payload \cite{mitzenmacher2004digital,zhao2018deepthings,zeng2020coedge}. Nevertheless, these techniques have largely been developed independently of UAV mobility and have not been jointly optimized with realistic A2G channel constraints at the packet level.

\noindent\textbf{Summary and Proposed Framework}
In summary, the existing literature typically addresses the three pillars independently: UAV trajectory optimization focuses on mobility, edge cooperation emphasizes distributed inference, and fragmented delivery concentrates on data structure. 

Building upon these foundations, this paper presents a coordinated framework that integrates the three key elements---recognition payload fragmentation, coverage-aware UAV trajectory planning, and edge cooperation---under realistic packet-level PHY constraints (short contact windows and stochastic PER). Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed framework significantly reduces mission-level time-to-detection, particularly under stringent A2G channel conditions.

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}
\subsection{System Model}
\noindent\textbf{Network Model.}
We consider an urban IoT system with a base station (BS), a UAV, and a set of ground nodes $\mathcal{N}$ distributed over a monitoring region and organized into cells for localized cooperation.

A subset $\mathcal{P} \subseteq \mathcal{N}$ represents candidate nodes associated with a potential event, among which an unknown node $n^*$ must accumulate sufficient evidence for detection. The mission is completed when $n^*$ reaches a predefined confidence threshold.

Each node can receive fragments from the UAV, exchange information with neighbors, and maintain a local confidence score.

\noindent\textbf{UAV Mobility and Coverage Model.}
The UAV flies at a fixed altitude \(h\) and follows a continuous waypoint sequence \(\Pi = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_M\}\) at constant speed \(v\) without hovering. Fragments are broadcast \emph{continuously} along the entire trajectory while the UAV is in motion. Coverage at any time \(t\) is determined by the instantaneous position of the UAV and a broadcast radius \(R_b\) derived from the A2G link budget.

Let \(\mathrm{CS}(t) \subseteq \mathcal{P}\) denote the set of suspicious nodes covered by the UAV at time \(t\). The trajectory is planned over a candidate set of locations (e.g., node positions or cell centroids) to maximize coverage efficiency while respecting mobility constraints.

The instantaneous Euclidean distance between the UAV and node \(n\) at time \(t\) is
\begin{equation}
d_n(t) = \sqrt{(x_{\mathrm{uav}}(t) - x_n)^2 + (y_{\mathrm{uav}}(t) - y_n)^2 + h^2}.
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Data Dissemination and Cooperation Model.}
A recognition payload is partitioned into \(K\) fragments. The UAV continuously broadcasts these fragments during flight. Let \(\mathcal{F}_n(t)\) denote the set of fragments successfully received by node \(n\) up to time \(t\).

Ground nodes engage in cooperative exchange (both intra-cell and inter-cell via designated gateways) to share received fragments and accelerate confidence accumulation at the suspicion point. The detailed triggering conditions and exchange protocols are specified in the coordination algorithms presented in Section~V.

\noindent\textbf{Fragment and Detection Model.}
Each fragment \(i\) provides an independent evidence contribution \(p_i \in (0,1)\). 
The confidence at node \(n\) accumulates over received fragments as
\begin{equation}
C_n(t) = 1 - \prod_{i \in \mathcal{F}_n(t)} (1 - p_i),
\end{equation}
which captures the union of independent evidence.

In practice, this accumulation can be implemented in a log-odds form for numerical stability and efficient incremental updates, where each fragment contributes additively to the confidence score.

\noindent\textbf{Communication and PHY Model.}
The UAV--node link follows a standard air-to-ground (A2G) channel model that accounts for distance-dependent path loss, log-normal shadowing, and small-scale fading \cite{alhourani2014modeling}. The received power at node \(n\) at time \(t\) is
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{rx}}(t) = P_{\mathrm{tx}} - \mathrm{PL}(d_n(t)) - X_{\sigma}(t) - X_f(t),
\end{equation}
where \(P_{\mathrm{tx}}\) is the transmit power, \(\mathrm{PL}(\cdot)\) is the path-loss function, \(X_{\sigma}(t)\) is log-normal shadowing, and \(X_f(t)\) is small-scale fading.

The instantaneous signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) determines the bit error rate (BER). This BER is mapped to packet error rate (PER) via
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PER} = 1 - (1 - \mathrm{BER})^{8L},
\end{equation}
where \(L\) is the packet size in bytes \cite{alhourani2014modeling}. A packet is successfully received only if (i) \(P_{\mathrm{rx}}(t) \ge P_{\mathrm{sens}}\) (receiver sensitivity threshold) and (ii) the condition holds throughout the entire packet transmission duration.

\noindent\textbf{Timing and Contact Model.}
Let \(t_{\mathrm{tx}} = 8L / R\) denote the transmission time of one packet, where \(R\) is the data rate. Because the UAV broadcasts continuously while moving, a \emph{contact window} between the UAV and node \(n\) is defined as the maximal continuous time interval during which \(P_{\mathrm{rx}}(t) \ge P_{\mathrm{sens}}\). All fragment deliveries and cooperative exchanges must complete within these valid contact windows. Scheduling decisions therefore incorporate both instantaneous channel feasibility and per-node buffer constraints.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Let \(T_{\mathrm{detect}}\) be the earliest time at which the suspicion point \(n^*\) reaches the alert threshold:
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{detect}} = \inf \{ t \mid C_{n^*}(t) \ge \tau_{\mathrm{alert}} \}.
\end{equation}

The objective is to minimize the mission-level detection time:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\Pi,\, \mathcal{S}} \quad T_{\mathrm{detect}},
\end{equation}
where \(\Pi\) is the UAV waypoint sequence and \(\mathcal{S}\) is the joint scheduling policy for fragment broadcasting and cooperative exchange. The optimization is subject to UAV mobility constraints (speed and trajectory continuity), communication constraints (contact windows, PER, and receiver sensitivity), intra- and inter-cell cooperation limits, and per-node buffer/scheduling constraints.
\section{Proposed Approach}

\noindent\textbf{Overview.}
The proposed coordinated dissemination framework integrates three tightly coupled modules that operate in a closed loop: (i) coverage-aware UAV trajectory planning, (ii) continuous fragment broadcasting, and (iii) distributed edge cooperation. The base station (BS) first constructs a globally near-optimal UAV path using a greedy maximum-coverage (GMC) heuristic. While the UAV flies along this path, it continuously broadcasts recognition fragments. Ground nodes update their local confidence scores upon successful reception and trigger cooperative exchanges when necessary to accelerate evidence accumulation at the suspicion point \(n^*\).

The framework takes as input the network topology, suspicious-node set \(\mathcal{P}\), threshold parameters \(\tau_{\text{coop}}\) and \(\tau_{\text{alert}}\), and PHY settings. It outputs the UAV waypoint sequence \(\Pi\), the transmission schedule \(\mathcal{S}\), and the mission completion time \(T_{\text{detect}}\). A heuristic design is adopted because the joint optimization of trajectory, scheduling, and random packet outcomes is computationally intractable for real-time missions.

\noindent\textbf{Cell Formation and Local Coordination Structure.}

To support efficient localized cooperation under limited communication range, ground nodes are organized into geographic cells. This cell structure reduces coordination overhead, limits contention, and enables scalable fragment exchange within bounded neighborhoods.

Fig.~\ref{fig:cell_and_tree} illustrates the resulting cell-based topology, where each cell operates as a local coordination domain with a designated leader and gateway nodes for inter-cell communication.

The cell formation and leader election procedure is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:cell}. Nodes are grouped based on spatial proximity, and a lightweight distributed selection mechanism is used to assign coordination roles within each cell.

\noindent\textbf{UAV Trajectory Planning (GMC).}
To achieve efficient spatial coverage with minimal flight distance, we employ a greedy maximum-coverage (GMC) heuristic. Candidate waypoints are selected from node positions and cell centroids. The algorithm iteratively chooses the waypoint that provides the highest coverage gain per unit travel cost while considering cell-level coverage (if a waypoint covers more than a threshold fraction \(\alpha\) of a cell, the entire cell is marked as covered). The resulting trajectory \(\Pi\) balances coverage efficiency and travel cost.

The pseudocode of the GMC planner is given in Algorithm~\ref{alg:gmc}.

\noindent\textbf{Fragment Broadcasting and Confidence Update.}
Along the planned trajectory \(\Pi\), the UAV continuously broadcasts the \(K\) fragments in a cyclic manner without hovering. Each ground node \(n\) maintains a local set of received fragments \(\mathcal{F}_n(t)\) and updates its confidence score \(C_n(t)\) incrementally whenever a new fragment is successfully received (using either the union-probability or log-odds form presented in Section~III).

\noindent\textbf{Cooperative Edge Protocol.}
To compensate for packet losses caused by short contact windows, nodes engage in both intra-cell and inter-cell cooperation. Cooperation is triggered by three events: (i) local confidence exceeding \(\tau_{\text{coop}}\), (ii) the end of a UAV contact window, or (iii) receipt of a manifest revealing missing fragments. Upon triggering, a node broadcasts a compact manifest and exchanges missing fragments with peers. Cell gateways handle inter-cell forwarding when necessary, using timeout and jitter mechanisms to avoid collisions.

The complete cooperative exchange logic is detailed in Algorithm~\ref{alg:coop}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{End-to-End Coordinated Detection Workflow}
\label{alg:workflow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Topology $\mathcal{G}$, suspicious set $\mathcal{P}$, suspicion point $n^*$, payload $\mathcal{D}$, PHY parameters
\Ensure UAV path $\Pi$, schedule $\mathcal{S}$, detection time $T_{\text{detect}}$

\State \textbf{Initial Setup:} Organize ground nodes into cells $\{\mathcal{C}_i\}$ and elect cell leaders
\State \textbf{Precompute Coverage:} Build candidate set and coverage map over $\mathcal{P}$
\State \textbf{Data Preparation:} Partition $\mathcal{D}$ into $K$ fragments $\{f_i\}$ with uniform information content

\State \textbf{Path Planning:} Compute UAV trajectory $\Pi$ using GMC-based heuristic
\State \textbf{Initialize:} Set confidence $C_n \gets 0$ for all nodes

\For{each UAV waypoint $w \in \Pi$}
    \State Broadcast fragments according to schedule $\mathcal{S}$
    \For{each node $n$ receiving fragment $f_i$}
        \State Update confidence $C_n \gets C_n + \Delta(f_i)$

        \If{$C_n \ge \tau_{\text{coop}}$}
            \State Broadcast manifest and request missing fragments from peers
            \State Receive fragments and update $C_n$
        \EndIf

        \If{$n = n^*$ and $C_n \ge \tau_{\text{alert}}$}
            \State \Return $T_{\text{detect}}$
        \EndIf
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Coverage-Aware UAV Path Planning (GMC)}
\label{alg:gmc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Candidate set $\mathcal{C}$, suspicious set $\mathcal{P}$
\Ensure Ordered waypoint list $\Pi$

\State Initialize $\Pi \gets \emptyset$, $\mathcal{P}_{\text{covered}} \gets \emptyset$

\While{$\mathcal{P}_{\text{covered}} \neq \mathcal{P}$}
    \For{each candidate $c \in \mathcal{C}$}
        \State Compute coverage gain $G(c)$ over uncovered nodes
        \If{$c$ covers $> \alpha$ fraction of a cell}
            \State Mark entire cell as covered
        \EndIf
        \State Compute cost $L(c)$ (travel distance)
        \State Score $S(c) \gets \frac{G(c)}{L(c)^\alpha + \epsilon}$
    \EndFor

    \State Select $c^* = \arg\max S(c)$
    \State Append $c^*$ to $\Pi$
    \State Update $\mathcal{P}_{\text{covered}}$
\EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Cooperative Exchange (Intra- and Inter-cell)}
\label{alg:coop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Node $n$, fragment set $\mathcal{F}_n$, thresholds $\tau_{\text{coop}},\tau_{\text{alert}}$, CGW, NGW
\Ensure Updated fragment sets and confidence

\State Trigger := ( $C_n \ge \tau_{\text{coop}}$ ) 
       OR (UAV contact ended) 
       OR (peer manifest reveals missing fragments)

\If{Trigger}
    \State Broadcast manifest; receive requests; exchange missing fragments with neighbors (with collision-aware scheduling)
    \State Update $\mathcal{F}_n$ and $C_n$
\EndIf

\If{CGW detects stagnation (no new manifests) AND $\exists n: C_n < \tau_{\text{coop}}$}
    \If{NGW has missing fragments AND target nodes satisfy $C_n < \tau_{\text{alert}}$ (after timeout)}
        \State Request and forward fragments via gateway-to-gateway coordination
        \State Update fragment sets and confidences across cells
    \EndIf
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Metrics}
To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed fragment-based cooperative detection and GMC planning, we employ the following metrics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mission Completion Time ($T_{\text{complete}}$)}: The primary metric, defined as the time interval from UAV flight initialization until the designated suspicion point reaches the alert threshold $\tau_{\text{alert}}$.
    \item \textbf{UAV Path Length}: The cumulative distance traveled by the UAV along its waypoint sequence until the mission is completed.
    \item \textbf{Cooperation Gain}: The ratio of fragments received through intra-cell cooperative exchange versus those received directly from UAV broadcasts. This measures the effectiveness of the cell layer in compensating for link failures.
    \item \textbf{Packet Delivery Ratio (PDR)}: The fraction of successfully received fragment packets over the total number of packets broadcast by the UAV, illustrating the impact of mobility-aware PHY constraints.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
The results in Fig.~\ref{fig:uav2-completion} show a clear performance gap across strategies. Coordinated fragment dissemination consistently achieves significantly shorter detection times than the nearest-neighbor (NN) baseline, with the advantage becoming more pronounced as network size increases. This suggests that mission efficiency depends more on how quickly useful information is exposed to multiple nodes, rather than on sequential node visits.

The perfect-channel variant is used as an upper bound to isolate packet-level impairments. Despite a measured packet loss rate of approximately 35.3%, the proposed method shows only a small gap compared to the ideal case, indicating that performance is largely driven by dissemination and fusion strategies rather than channel conditions alone.

Regarding UAV trajectory, Fig.~\ref{fig:uav-path-three} shows that coverage-aware (GMC-based) planning produces shorter and more structured paths than NN. The proposed cell-aware variant further reduces travel distance while maintaining effective coverage, improving overall movement efficiency.

The benefit of edge cooperation is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:conf-events-stacked}. The suspicion point can accumulate useful information from neighboring nodes even before direct UAV contact, and a substantial portion of the final confidence comes from shared fragments. This highlights the role of cooperation in amplifying information, especially in larger networks.

From a system perspective, the results indicate a shift in design strategy: instead of attempting full payload delivery over short and unreliable A2G links, continuous fragment dissemination combined with local cooperation enables steady information accumulation over time.

Finally, the path-length results reveal a key trade-off. The proposed method may incur slightly higher detection time than classical GMC in some cases, but significantly reduces UAV travel distance, offering a better balance between latency and mobility cost.

Overall, the results support the central premise of this work: jointly coordinating fragment dissemination, coverage-aware trajectory planning, and edge cooperation is an effective approach to reducing detection latency under realistic A2G constraints.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper studied the problem of minimizing time-to-detection in UAV-assisted urban IoT systems under realistic packet-level constraints. We proposed a coordinated framework that jointly integrates payload fragmentation, coverage-aware UAV trajectory planning, and edge cooperation. By enabling continuous fragment dissemination and local exchange among nodes, the system accelerates confidence accumulation at the true detection point without requiring full payload delivery.

Built upon a realistic A2G channel model with path loss, shadowing, fading, and packet errors, the proposed design is suitable for harsh urban environments with limited contact time. Simulation results demonstrate that the framework significantly reduces detection latency and UAV travel cost compared to conventional approaches, while highlighting the importance of cooperation in mitigating unreliable links.

Overall, the results show that jointly optimizing dissemination, mobility, and cooperation is key to achieving low-latency detection. Future work includes adaptive trajectory design, optimized cooperation control, multi-UAV extensions, and integration with real edge-AI models.
