\begin{abstract}
Urban edge-camera networks and IoT sensing infrastructures are increasingly deployed to support time-critical monitoring applications, where rapid target detection is essential in emergency scenarios. In such settings, Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) can serve as mobile data ferries to disseminate high-value recognition payloads (e.g., images or feature representations) to spatially distributed ground nodes. However, transmitting large payloads is fundamentally constrained by short air-to-ground (A2G) contact windows and packet-level impairments such as path loss, shadowing, and stochastic packet errors (PER).

This paper addresses the problem of minimizing mission-level time-to-detection under realistic PHY-layer constraints. The key challenge lies in delivering useful recognition information to ground nodes covering target areas despite limited contact duration and stochastic packet losses, which make complete payload transfer impractical.

We propose a coordinated dissemination framework that integrates three complementary mechanisms under realistic PHY-layer constraints. Recognition payloads are partitioned into small fragments with approximately uniform information content, each contributing partial evidence toward detection. A UAV continuously broadcasts these fragments along a coverage-aware trajectory, while ground nodes cooperatively exchange and fuse fragments through localized collaboration. Unlike conventional UAV-only delivery approaches, our design explicitly coordinates fragment broadcasting with distributed edge cooperation and enforces packet-level feasibility.

Simulation results show that the proposed framework significantly reduces mission-level time-to-detection compared to baseline strategies based on monolithic transmission or UAV-only delivery, particularly under stringent A2G conditions with short contact windows and high packet error rates. The results highlight the importance of jointly optimizing fine-grained dissemination, edge cooperation, and PHY-aware communication in UAV-assisted urban sensing systems.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In recent years, UAV-assisted IoT applications have developed rapidly and become an important solution in many real-world domains. UAVs serve as flexible mobile platforms that support environmental monitoring, industrial data collection, smart agriculture, and urban intelligent systems. Thanks to their high-altitude flight and wide coverage capability, UAVs enable connectivity with spatially distributed IoT sensor nodes over large areas without relying entirely on fixed infrastructure.

The target detection problem is one of the most critical applications of UAV-assisted IoT systems. This application can serve numerous practical scenarios, including emergency search-and-rescue, environmental monitoring, urban security, and disaster response. Specifically, a wide target area containing hundreds of IoT sensor nodes must receive a high-quality recognition payload (such as images or video) from the UAV. Within this area lies a suspicion point adjacent to a particular node that needs to detect the target. The core task is to ensure that this suspicion node acquires sufficient recognition data as early as possible, thereby enabling low-latency detection decisions.

Traditional methods typically adopt a straightforward approach: the UAV flies close to each node and transmits the entire payload in a single session. However, this strategy requires the UAV to fly slowly or hover for extended periods to guarantee complete data delivery, resulting in significantly prolonged mission completion time. Moreover, realistic physical-layer (PHY) constraints must be considered, such as short air-to-ground contact windows, path loss, shadowing, and stochastic packet errors (PER). These factors not only increase the UAV’s energy consumption but also substantially reduce overall system efficiency. Therefore, a faster and more effective approach is needed.

We propose a new comprehensive coordinated approach. Instead of transmitting a large monolithic payload, the UAV disseminates only a small number of fragments, each carrying comparable recognition value. These fragments are broadcast continuously while the UAV is in motion, combined with an efficient trajectory strategy that maximizes coverage of the target area without unnecessary overlaps. At the same time, the ground IoT nodes collaborate closely through edge fusion and selective fragment exchange. This enables the suspicion node to accumulate sufficient detection evidence much earlier without requiring the UAV to deliver the complete payload to every individual node.

This approach is built upon a clear understanding of the air-to-ground (A2G) channel and realistic PHY constraints. Specifically, the system accounts for UAV-node distance, distance-dependent path loss, shadowing, and fading effects when deciding the timing and manner of fragment transmission, ensuring feasibility at the packet level under harsh urban channel conditions.

The core ideas of the proposed solution include payload fragmentation, organization of nodes into cell layers to support localized cooperation, selective fragment broadcasting along an optimized trajectory, and an edge cooperation mechanism that allows nodes to efficiently exchange missing fragments. These components are tightly integrated within a single coordination framework, significantly reducing target detection time while keeping the UAV’s flight path cost low.

To the best of our knowledge, the solution proposed in this paper is the first work that simultaneously integrates three key elements: recognition payload fragmentation, coverage-aware UAV trajectory planning, and edge cooperation under realistic packet-level PHY constraints.

Section~IV formulates the mission-time minimization problem. Section~V presents the proposed coordination protocol in detail. Section~VI evaluates performance through extensive simulations. Section~VII discusses limitations and future extensions, and Section~VIII concludes the paper.
\section{Related Work}

\subsection{UAV-Assisted Dissemination and AoI-Aware Scheduling}
Recent studies have widely employed UAVs as mobile platforms (data ferries) or aerial base stations to support data collection and dissemination in IoT networks \cite{wei2022uav,nguyen2021uav}. These works primarily focus on optimizing UAV trajectories to improve coverage, Age-of-Information (AoI), or mission completion time \cite{liu2018age,abd2020deep}. However, most approaches assume monolithic payloads and idealized channel models (binary in-range), overlooking realistic physical-layer constraints such as short contact windows and stochastic packet error rates (PER) in urban air-to-ground (A2G) environments. Consequently, these solutions are often unsuitable for transmitting high-quality recognition payloads under harsh channel conditions.

\subsection{Distributed Edge Detection and Cooperative Inference}
In a parallel line of research, distributed edge inference and cooperative detection aim to enhance detection accuracy through local information exchange and consensus mechanisms \cite{blum2002distributed,ren2023survey}. Recent works have also explored early-exit techniques and confidence-threshold mechanisms to reduce decision latency \cite{teerapittayanon2016branchynet}. While these approaches are effective in environments with stable infrastructure, they typically assume negligible communication overhead and continuous connectivity. In the context of UAV flyovers with extremely short contact durations, these assumptions no longer hold, rendering edge cooperation inefficient or infeasible without proper coordination with the UAV dissemination process.

\subsection{Fragmented/Progressive Data Delivery}
A third research direction investigates progressive or fragmented data delivery, a concept long studied for bandwidth-limited channels \cite{tzou1987progressive,chee1999survey}. Recent studies have demonstrated that even when images or feature maps are fragmented, each fragment retains meaningful recognition value and can be flexibly fused using confidence accumulation, Bayesian fusion, or log-odds methods \cite{feng2019hierarchical,chen2020progressive,gandor2022compression}. In edge-AI contexts, feature-map partitioning further enables early detection without requiring the full payload \cite{mitzenmacher2004digital,zhao2018deepthings,zeng2020coedge}. Nevertheless, these techniques have largely been developed independently of UAV mobility and have not been jointly optimized with realistic A2G channel constraints at the packet level.

\subsection{Summary and Proposed Framework}
In summary, the existing literature typically addresses the three pillars independently: UAV trajectory optimization focuses on mobility, edge cooperation emphasizes distributed inference, and fragmented delivery concentrates on data structure. 

Building upon these foundations, this paper presents a coordinated framework that integrates the three key elements---recognition payload fragmentation, coverage-aware UAV trajectory planning, and edge cooperation---under realistic packet-level PHY constraints (short contact windows and stochastic PER). Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed framework significantly reduces mission-level time-to-detection, particularly under stringent A2G channel conditions.

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}

\subsection{Network Model}
We consider an urban IoT sensing system consisting of a base station (BS), a single UAV, and a set of ground nodes \(\mathcal{N}\). The nodes are spatially distributed over a monitoring region and organized into disjoint geographic cells to enable localized cooperation \cite{blum2002distributed,ren2023survey}.

The BS identifies a subset of suspicious nodes \(\mathcal{P} \subseteq \mathcal{N}\) that may contain a target of interest. The actual \emph{suspicion point} \(n^*\) (the node adjacent to the target that must accumulate sufficient evidence) is chosen uniformly at random from \(\mathcal{P}\) and is unknown to all network entities at the beginning of the mission. The mission is considered complete once the suspicion point \(n^*\) has accumulated enough detection evidence.

Each node \(n \in \mathcal{N}\) is equipped with local storage and can: 
(i) receive data fragments from the UAV, 
(ii) exchange fragments with neighboring nodes (intra-cell or inter-cell via gateways), and 
(iii) maintain a local confidence score for detection.

\subsection{UAV Mobility and Coverage Model}
The UAV flies at a fixed altitude \(h\) and follows a continuous waypoint sequence \(\Pi = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_M\}\) at constant speed \(v\) without hovering. Fragments are broadcast \emph{continuously} along the entire trajectory while the UAV is in motion. Coverage at any time \(t\) is determined by the instantaneous position of the UAV and a broadcast radius \(R_b\) derived from the A2G link budget.

Let \(\mathrm{CS}(t) \subseteq \mathcal{P}\) denote the set of suspicious nodes covered by the UAV at time \(t\). The trajectory is planned over a candidate set of locations (e.g., node positions or cell centroids) to maximize coverage efficiency while respecting mobility constraints.

The instantaneous Euclidean distance between the UAV and node \(n\) at time \(t\) is
\begin{equation}
d_n(t) = \sqrt{(x_{\mathrm{uav}}(t) - x_n)^2 + (y_{\mathrm{uav}}(t) - y_n)^2 + h^2}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Data Dissemination and Cooperation Model}
A recognition payload is partitioned into \(K\) fragments. The UAV continuously broadcasts these fragments during flight. Let \(\mathcal{F}_n(t)\) denote the set of fragments successfully received by node \(n\) up to time \(t\).

Ground nodes engage in cooperative exchange (both intra-cell and inter-cell via designated gateways) to share received fragments and accelerate confidence accumulation at the suspicion point. The detailed triggering conditions and exchange protocols are specified in the coordination algorithms presented in Section~V.

\subsection{Fragment and Detection Model}
Each fragment \(i\) contributes an independent partial evidence \(p_i \in (0,1)\) toward target detection. 
The confidence score at node \(n\) is defined as
\begin{equation}
C_n(t) = 1 - \prod_{i \in \mathcal{F}_n(t)} (1 - p_i),
\end{equation}
which follows the union-probability accumulation rule under the assumption of independent fragment contributions.

An equivalent and numerically stable log-odds representation is
\begin{equation}
C_n(t) = \sigma\!\left( \sum_{i \in \mathcal{F}_n(t)} \log \frac{p_i}{1-p_i} \right),
\end{equation}
where \(\sigma(\cdot)\) is the sigmoid function. This formulation treats each successfully received fragment as an independent piece of evidence and corresponds to Bayesian updating of the posterior probability under a neutral prior \cite{blum2002distributed}. It enables efficient incremental updates and avoids numerical underflow that can occur with the direct product form when the number of fragments is large.

\subsection{Communication and PHY Model}
The UAV--node link follows a standard air-to-ground (A2G) channel model that accounts for distance-dependent path loss, log-normal shadowing, and small-scale fading \cite{alhourani2014modeling}. The received power at node \(n\) at time \(t\) is
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{rx}}(t) = P_{\mathrm{tx}} - \mathrm{PL}(d_n(t)) - X_{\sigma}(t) - X_f(t),
\end{equation}
where \(P_{\mathrm{tx}}\) is the transmit power, \(\mathrm{PL}(\cdot)\) is the path-loss function, \(X_{\sigma}(t)\) is log-normal shadowing, and \(X_f(t)\) is small-scale fading.

The instantaneous signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) determines the bit error rate (BER). This BER is mapped to packet error rate (PER) via
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PER} = 1 - (1 - \mathrm{BER})^{8L},
\end{equation}
where \(L\) is the packet size in bytes \cite{alhourani2014modeling}. A packet is successfully received only if (i) \(P_{\mathrm{rx}}(t) \ge P_{\mathrm{sens}}\) (receiver sensitivity threshold) and (ii) the condition holds throughout the entire packet transmission duration.

\subsection{Timing and Contact Model}
Let \(t_{\mathrm{tx}} = 8L / R\) denote the transmission time of one packet, where \(R\) is the data rate. Because the UAV broadcasts continuously while moving, a \emph{contact window} between the UAV and node \(n\) is defined as the maximal continuous time interval during which \(P_{\mathrm{rx}}(t) \ge P_{\mathrm{sens}}\). All fragment deliveries and cooperative exchanges must complete within these valid contact windows. Scheduling decisions therefore incorporate both instantaneous channel feasibility and per-node buffer constraints.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Let \(T_{\mathrm{detect}}\) be the earliest time at which the suspicion point \(n^*\) reaches the alert threshold:
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{detect}} = \inf \{ t \mid C_{n^*}(t) \ge \tau_{\mathrm{alert}} \}.
\end{equation}

The objective is to minimize the mission-level detection time:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\Pi,\, \mathcal{S}} \quad T_{\mathrm{detect}},
\end{equation}
where \(\Pi\) is the UAV waypoint sequence and \(\mathcal{S}\) is the joint scheduling policy for fragment broadcasting and cooperative exchange. The optimization is subject to UAV mobility constraints (speed and trajectory continuity), communication constraints (contact windows, PER, and receiver sensitivity), intra- and inter-cell cooperation limits, and per-node buffer/scheduling constraints.
\section{Proposed Approach}

\subsection{Overview}
The proposed coordinated dissemination framework integrates three tightly coupled modules that operate in a closed loop: (i) coverage-aware UAV trajectory planning, (ii) continuous fragment broadcasting, and (iii) distributed edge cooperation. The base station (BS) first constructs a globally near-optimal UAV path using a greedy maximum-coverage (GMC) heuristic. While the UAV flies along this path, it continuously broadcasts recognition fragments. Ground nodes update their local confidence scores upon successful reception and trigger cooperative exchanges when necessary to accelerate evidence accumulation at the suspicion point \(n^*\).

The framework takes as input the network topology, suspicious-node set \(\mathcal{P}\), threshold parameters \(\tau_{\text{coop}}\) and \(\tau_{\text{alert}}\), and PHY settings. It outputs the UAV waypoint sequence \(\Pi\), the transmission schedule \(\mathcal{S}\), and the mission completion time \(T_{\text{detect}}\). A heuristic design is adopted because the joint optimization of trajectory, scheduling, and random packet outcomes is computationally intractable for real-time missions.

\subsection{UAV Trajectory Planning (GMC)}
To achieve efficient spatial coverage with minimal flight distance, we employ a greedy maximum-coverage (GMC) heuristic. Candidate waypoints are selected from node positions and cell centroids. The algorithm iteratively chooses the waypoint that provides the highest coverage gain per unit travel cost while considering cell-level coverage (if a waypoint covers more than a threshold fraction \(\theta\) of a cell, the entire cell is marked as covered). The resulting trajectory \(\Pi\) balances coverage efficiency and travel cost.

The pseudocode of the GMC planner is given in Algorithm~\ref{alg:gmc}.

\subsection{Fragment Broadcasting and Confidence Update}
Along the planned trajectory \(\Pi\), the UAV continuously broadcasts the \(K\) fragments in a cyclic manner without hovering. Each ground node \(n\) maintains a local set of received fragments \(\mathcal{F}_n(t)\) and updates its confidence score \(C_n(t)\) incrementally whenever a new fragment is successfully received (using either the union-probability or log-odds form presented in Section~III).

\subsection{Cooperative Edge Protocol}
To compensate for packet losses caused by short contact windows, nodes engage in both intra-cell and inter-cell cooperation. Cooperation is triggered by three events: (i) local confidence exceeding \(\tau_{\text{coop}}\), (ii) the end of a UAV contact window, or (iii) receipt of a manifest revealing missing fragments. Upon triggering, a node broadcasts a compact manifest and exchanges missing fragments with peers. Cell gateways handle inter-cell forwarding when necessary, using timeout and jitter mechanisms to avoid collisions.

The complete cooperative exchange logic is detailed in Algorithm~\ref{alg:coop}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{End-to-End Coordinated Detection Workflow}
\label{alg:workflow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Topology $\mathcal{G}$, suspicious set $\mathcal{P}$, suspicion point $n^*$, payload $\mathcal{D}$, PHY parameters
\Ensure UAV path $\Pi$, schedule $\mathcal{S}$, detection time $T_{\text{detect}}$

\State \textbf{Initial Setup:} Organize ground nodes into cells $\{\mathcal{C}_i\}$ and elect cell leaders
\State \textbf{Precompute Coverage:} Build candidate set and coverage map over $\mathcal{P}$
\State \textbf{Data Preparation:} Partition $\mathcal{D}$ into $K$ fragments $\{f_i\}$ with uniform information content

\State \textbf{Path Planning:} Compute UAV trajectory $\Pi$ using GMC-based heuristic
\State \textbf{Initialize:} Set confidence $C_n \gets 0$ for all nodes

\For{each UAV waypoint $w \in \Pi$}
    \State Broadcast fragments according to schedule $\mathcal{S}$
    \For{each node $n$ receiving fragment $f_i$}
        \State Update confidence $C_n \gets C_n + \Delta(f_i)$

        \If{$C_n \ge \tau_{\text{coop}}$}
            \State Broadcast manifest and request missing fragments from peers
            \State Receive fragments and update $C_n$
        \EndIf

        \If{$n = n^*$ and $C_n \ge \tau_{\text{alert}}$}
            \State \Return $T_{\text{detect}}$
        \EndIf
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Cell Formation and Leader Election}
\label{alg:cell}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Node set $\mathcal{N}$ with positions
\Ensure Cell structure $\{\mathcal{C}_i\}$ with leaders

\State Partition nodes into cells based on geographic proximity
\For{each cell $\mathcal{C}_i$}
    \State Nodes discover neighbors within communication range
    \State Compute fitness score for leader selection (e.g., proximity to cell centroid)
    \State Elect node with highest score as cell leader
    \State Designate gateway nodes for inter-cell communication
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Coverage-Aware UAV Path Planning (GMC)}
\label{alg:gmc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Candidate set $\mathcal{C}$, suspicious set $\mathcal{P}$
\Ensure Ordered waypoint list $\Pi$

\State Initialize $\Pi \gets \emptyset$, $\mathcal{P}_{\text{covered}} \gets \emptyset$

\While{$\mathcal{P}_{\text{covered}} \neq \mathcal{P}$}
    \For{each candidate $c \in \mathcal{C}$}
        \State Compute coverage gain $G(c)$ over uncovered nodes
        \If{$c$ covers $> \theta$ fraction of a cell}
            \State Mark entire cell as covered
        \EndIf
        \State Compute cost $L(c)$ (travel distance)
        \State Score $S(c) \gets \frac{G(c)}{L(c)^\alpha + \epsilon}$
    \EndFor

    \State Select $c^* = \arg\max S(c)$
    \State Append $c^*$ to $\Pi$
    \State Update $\mathcal{P}_{\text{covered}}$
\EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Cooperative Exchange (Intra- and Inter-cell)}
\label{alg:coop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Node $n$, fragment set $\mathcal{F}_n$, thresholds $\tau_{\text{coop}},\tau_{\text{alert}}$, cell gateway CGW, neighbor gateway NGW
\Ensure Updated fragment set(s) and confidence

\State define Trigger := ( $C_n \ge \tau_{\text{coop}}$ )
\State \quad \quad \quad OR (UAV just left $\Rightarrow$ contact window ended)
\State \quad \quad \quad OR (received manifest from peer showing missing fragments)

\If{Trigger}
    \State Broadcast manifest (list of owned fragments)
    \State Receive requests from neighbors in same cell
    \State Exchange missing fragments with peers (apply stagger/jitter scheduling to avoid collisions)
    \State Update local fragment set $\mathcal{F}_n$ and confidence $C_n$
\EndIf

\State /* Cell gateway (CGW) logic for inter-cell cooperation */
\If{CGW observes (no new manifests in cell) AND some nodes with $C_n < \tau_{\text{coop}}$}
    \If{NGW indicates neighbor cell has fragments missing locally AND target nodes satisfy $C_n < \tau_{\text{alert}}$ AND timeout expired}
        \State CGW request missing fragments from NGW
        \State NGW coordinates transfer (gateway-to-gateway exchange) and forwards fragments to requesting nodes
        \State Update fragment sets and confidences in both cells
    \EndIf
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Metrics}
To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed fragment-based cooperative detection and GMC planning, we employ the following metrics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mission Completion Time ($T_{\text{complete}}$)}: The primary metric, defined as the time interval from UAV flight initialization until the designated suspicion point reaches the alert threshold $\tau_{\text{alert}}$.
    \item \textbf{UAV Path Length}: The cumulative distance traveled by the UAV along its waypoint sequence until the mission is completed.
    \item \textbf{Cooperation Gain}: The ratio of fragments received through intra-cell cooperative exchange versus those received directly from UAV broadcasts. This measures the effectiveness of the cell layer in compensating for link failures.
    \item \textbf{Packet Delivery Ratio (PDR)}: The fraction of successfully received fragment packets over the total number of packets broadcast by the UAV, illustrating the impact of mobility-aware PHY constraints.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}

The results in Fig.~\ref{fig:uav2-completion} provide a clear comparison of mission-level performance across strategies. The coordinated fragment dissemination approach consistently achieves significantly shorter detection times than the nearest-neighbor (NN) baseline across all network sizes. This trend suggests that mission efficiency is driven less by sequentially visiting nodes and more by how effectively the UAV can expose multiple suspicious nodes to useful information early in the mission. In this context, broad dissemination of partial information is more beneficial than exhaustive point-to-point delivery when the objective is early detection.

The UAV trajectory also plays a critical role in overall performance. As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:uav-path-three}, coverage-aware planning produces substantially shorter and more structured flight paths compared to the NN strategy. By reducing unnecessary detours and prioritizing high-coverage regions, the UAV can utilize limited contact opportunities more efficiently. This directly translates into reduced mission completion time, particularly when fragment broadcasting is performed continuously during motion.

The benefit of edge cooperation is further highlighted in Fig.~\ref{fig:conf-events-stacked}, which shows the accumulation of confidence over different phases of the mission. Cooperative exchange enables nodes to leverage fragment diversity from their neighbors, allowing the suspicion point to reach the detection threshold earlier than relying on direct UAV delivery alone. In larger networks, where fragment diversity across nearby nodes is higher, this effect becomes more pronounced. The stacked confidence contributions indicate that peer-assisted sharing accounts for a non-trivial portion of the final detection evidence.

However, the gain from cooperation is not uniform across all scenarios. In some configurations, the non-cooperative GMC variant performs comparably to the full cooperative scheme. This suggests that the effectiveness of cooperation depends on the balance between the benefit of fragment diversity and the overhead introduced by local exchange, gateway coordination, and channel contention. When fragment diversity is limited or communication overhead is high, the marginal gain of cooperation may diminish.

Another key observation is the impact of realistic PHY-layer conditions. The measured packet drop rate (approximately 35.3\%) indicates that a substantial fraction of transmitted packets are lost under the simulated A2G channel. This is consistent with the performance gap observed between the realistic and perfect-channel settings in Fig.~\ref{fig:uav2-completion}. Packet losses significantly reduce the effective data delivery rate, especially during short contact windows, and become more pronounced as the network scales. These results reinforce the importance of modeling packet-level feasibility rather than relying on idealized coverage assumptions.

From a system perspective, the results highlight a fundamental shift in design strategy. Instead of attempting to deliver complete payloads under unreliable and short-duration links, fragment-based dissemination allows the system to preserve useful detection progress even in the presence of packet loss. Combined with in-motion broadcasting and localized cooperation, this approach enables the network to convert intermittent A2G connectivity into steady accumulation of detection evidence.

The path-length results further illustrate an important trade-off. The proposed approach achieves earlier detection with shorter UAV travel distance compared to the NN baseline, implying both lower latency and reduced flight effort. This suggests that effective information dissemination does not require the UAV to visit every node closely; instead, coordinated broadcasting and edge cooperation can achieve broader coverage with less movement.

Finally, several limitations of the current evaluation should be noted. The variability across simulation runs remains non-negligible, reflecting sensitivity to stochastic packet loss and the spatial distribution of the suspicion point. In addition, the current experiments focus on a fixed set of parameters. A more comprehensive sensitivity analysis over fragment granularity, cooperation thresholds, UAV speed, and channel conditions would further clarify the operating regimes in which each mechanism provides the greatest benefit.

Overall, the results support the central premise of this work: jointly coordinating fragment dissemination, coverage-aware UAV motion, and localized edge cooperation provides a practical and effective approach to reducing mission-level detection latency under realistic A2G constraints.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper investigated the problem of minimizing mission-level time-to-detection in UAV-assisted urban IoT sensing systems under realistic packet-level communication constraints. Unlike conventional approaches that rely on monolithic payload delivery or UAV-only dissemination, the proposed framework jointly combines three key mechanisms: payload fragmentation, coverage-aware UAV trajectory planning, and localized edge cooperation among ground nodes. By allowing the UAV to continuously broadcast useful fragments while in motion and enabling neighboring nodes to exchange missing evidence, the system can accelerate confidence accumulation at the true suspicion point without requiring complete payload transfer to every node.

The proposed design was developed under a realistic A2G communication model that incorporates distance-dependent path loss, shadowing, fading, receiver sensitivity, and packet error effects. This PHY-aware formulation makes the framework more suitable for harsh urban environments, where short contact windows often make full data delivery impractical. The performance evaluation, although preliminary, indicates that the coordinated approach can substantially reduce detection latency and UAV travel cost compared with conventional node-by-node delivery strategies, while also highlighting the practical role of edge cooperation in improving robustness to imperfect air-to-ground links.

Overall, the results support the main conclusion of this work: early target detection in UAV-assisted IoT networks benefits significantly from treating dissemination, mobility, and edge cooperation as a single joint design problem rather than three separate components.

Several directions remain for future work. First, the current heuristic trajectory design can be extended toward adaptive or learning-based policies that respond online to packet outcomes and confidence evolution. Second, the cooperation protocol can be refined to explicitly optimize the trade-off between cooperation gain and local communication overhead. Third, the present study considers a single-UAV setting; extending the framework to multiple UAVs with coordinated coverage and fragment scheduling is an important next step. Finally, integrating task-oriented fragment generation from real vision models would further strengthen the connection between the proposed communication framework and practical edge-AI target detection systems.
